** Solution

*** Annotated Rewriting

*Proposition (Sum of subspaces)*

Let $W$ and $\hat{W}$ be subspaces of a $\mathbb{F}$ vector space $V$. Then:

1. An element $v \in V$ lies in $W + \hat{W}$ iff there exists elements $w \in W$ and $\hat{w} \in \hat{W} : v = w + \hat{w}$.

2. If $W$ and $\hat{W}$ are finite dimensional, then $W + \hat{W}$ is also finite dimensional and

   $$dim(W + \hat{W}) = dim(W) + dim(\hat{W}) - dim(W \cap \hat{W})$$

*Proof.*

- Setup

  Let $U, W \in V$ be finite dimensional subspaces.

  Let $I := U \cap W$.

  Choose $B_I$ be basis of $I$.

- Extend Basis

  Extend $B_I$ to be basis of $U$:

  $$B_U = B_I \cup B_1$$

  Extend $B_I$ to be basis of $W$:

  $$B_W = B_I \cup B_2$$

  Observe:

  $$dim(U) = |B_U| = |B_I| + |B_1|$$

  $$dim(W) = |B_W| = |B_I| + |B_2|$$

- Claim 1: Span

  Claim, $B_I \cup B_1 \cup B_2$ spans $U + W$.

  Let $v \in U + W$, hence, $v = u + w : u \in U, w \in W$. $u$ can be written as a linear combination of basis $B_U = B_I + B_1$, and $w$ as a linear combination of $B_W = B_I + B_2$, hence, $u + w$ can be represented as a linear combination of $B_I \cup B_1 \cup B_2$. Hence, $B_I \cup B_1 \cup B_2$ spans $U + W$.

- Claim 2: Linear Independence

  Claim, $B_I \cup B_1 \cup B_2$ is linearly independent.

  Assume

  $$\sum_{b \in B_I} \alpha_b b + \sum_{b \in B_1} \beta_b b + \sum_{b \in B_2} \gamma_b b = 0$$

  Observe that

  $$\left( \sum_{b \in B_I} \alpha_b b + \sum_{b \in B_1} \beta_b b \right) + \left( \sum_{b \in b_2} \gamma_b b \right) = 0$$

  where first parenthesis is a vector in $U$ (as constructed with $B_I$ and $B_1$), while second paranthesis is a vector in $W$ (as constructed with $B_2$).

  $$\left( \sum_{b \in B_I} \alpha_b b + \sum_{b \in B_1} \beta_b b \right) = - \left( \sum_{b \in b_2} \gamma_b b \right)$$

  Hence, all scalars $\beta$ must be equal to zero.

  As $\beta$'s equal to zero, the original equality simplifies to:

  $$\sum_{b \in B_I} \alpha_b b + \sum_{b \in B_2} \gamma_b b = 0$$

  Where the left side is a linear combination of $W$ basis. This forces all $\gamma$ scalars to be equal to zero, due to linear independence of the basis.

  Hence, all coefficients are zero. Thus, the original vector combination of basis $B_I, B_1, B_2$ is linear independent.

- Conclusion

  As $B_I \cup B_1 \cup B_2$ spans $U + W$, and is linearly independent, it is a basis of $U + W$.

  Therefore,

  $$dim(U + W) = dim(U) + dim(W) - dim(U \cap W)$$

*** Exam Style

*Proof.*

Let $U_1$, $U_2$ be finite dimensional subspaces of $V$. Let $\left\{ w_1, \cdots, w_k \right\}$ be basis of $U_1 \cap U_2$. Extend to a basis $\left\{ w_1, \cdots, w_k, u_1 \cdots, u_p \right\}$ of $U_1$ and a basis $\left\{ w_1, \cdots, w_k, v_1, \cdots, v_q \right\}$ of $U_2$.

Claim: $B = \left\{ w_1, \cdots, w_k, u_1, \cdots, u_p, v_1, \cdots, v_q \right\}$ is basis of $U_1 + U_2$.

Spanning is immediate: any $x = x_1 + x_2 \in U_1 + U_2$ can be expanded to respective basis, giving a linear combination over $B$.

Linear independence: Suppose $\sum \alpha_i w_i + \sum \beta_j u_j + \sum \gamma_l v_l = 0$. Then,

$$\sum \gamma_l v_l = - \sum \alpha_i w_i - \sum \beta_j u_j \in U_1$$

The left side is in $U_2$, so its vector is in $U_1 \cap U_2$, hence expressible as $\sum \mu_i w_i$. Then $\sum \gamma_l v_l - \sum \mu_i w_i = 0$, and the linear independence of $U_2$ basis gives $\gamma_l = 0$ for all $l$. Substituting back, $\sum \alpha_i w_i + \sum \beta_i u_i = 0$, and the linear independence of $U_1$ basis gives $\alpha_i = \beta_j = 0$ for all $i, j$.

Thus, $|B| = k + p + q = m + n - k$, i.e. $dim(U_1 + U_2) = dim(U_1) + dim(U_2) - dim(U_1 \cap U_2)$.

*** Exam Style 2

Let $U_1, U_2$ be finite dimensional subspaces of $V$. Put $I := U_1 \cap U_2$ and choose a basis $B_I = \left\{ w_1, \cdots, w_k \right\}$ of $I$. Extend $B_I$ to basis $B_1$ and $B_2$:

$$B_{U_1} = \left\{ w_1, \cdots, w_k, u_1, \cdots, u_p \right\}, \quad B_{U_2} = \left\{ w_1, \cdots, w_k, v_1, \cdots, v_q \right\}$$

Consider $B = B_I \cup \left\{ u_1, \cdots, u_p \right\} \cup \left\{ v_1, \cdots, v_q \right\}$.

Every $x \in U_1 + U_2$ equals to $x_1 + x_2 : x_1 \in U_1, x_2 \in U_2$, so $B$ spans $U_1, U_2$.

For independence, suppose $\sum \alpha_i w_i + \sum \beta_j u_j + \sum \gamma_l v_l = 0$. Rearanging gives:

$$\sum \gamma_l v_l = - \left\{ \sum \alpha_i w_i + \sum \beta_j u_j \right\} \in U_1$$

but the left side is in $U_2$, so $\sum \gamma_l v_l \in U_1 \cap U_2 = I$. Hence, $\sum \gamma_l v_l = \sum \mu_i w_i$ for some scalars $\mu_i$. Thus $\sum \gamma_l v_l - \sum \mu_i w_i = 0$, and linear independence of $B_{U_2}$ forces $\gamma_l = \mu_i = 0$. Substituting back, $\sum \alpha_i w_i + \sum \beta_j u_j = 0$, and linear independence of $B_{U_1}$ forces $w_i = u_j = 0$. Therefore $B$ is a basis of $U_1 + U_2$.

Thus,

$$dim(U_1 + U_2) = k + p + q = (p + k) + (q + k) - k = |U_1| + |U_2| - |U_1 \cap U_2|$$

* Block C: Application/Testing
** Task
#+begin_quote
(d) Compute the kernel of the linear map $T: \mathbb{C}^2 \rightarrow \mathbb{C}^2$

$$T(z, w) := \begin{pmatrix} (1 + 5i)z + 2w \\ (8 + i)z + (1 - 3i)w \end{pmatrix}$$

Is the map injective? (3 Points)
#+end_quote

** Solution

\begin{align*}
A &=
\begin{bmatrix}
(1 + 5i) & (2)      \\
(8 + i)  & (1 - 3i) \\
\end{bmatrix} \\
A
\begin{bmatrix}
z \\
w \\
\end{bmatrix} &=
\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
0 \\
\end{bmatrix} \\
\end{align*}


\begin{align*}
det(A) &= (1 + 5i)(1 - 3i) - (2)(8 + i) \\
       &= 16 + 2i - 16 - 2i \\
       &= 0 \\
\end{align*}

As the $det(A) = 0$, this implies matrix is not invertable. Therefore,
- $T$ is not injective
- $ker(T) \neq 0$

\begin{align*}
\begin{bmatrix}
(1 + 5i) & (2)      \\
(8 + i)  & (1 - 3i) \\
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
z \\
w \\
\end{bmatrix} &=
\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
0 \\
\end{bmatrix} \\
\end{align*}

$$\frac{(8 + i)}{(1 + 5i)} = \frac{(8 + i)(1 - 5i)}{1^{2} - 25i^{2}} = \frac{13 - 39i}{26} = \frac{1 - 3i}{2}$$

\begin{align*}
(1 - 3i) - (\frac{1 - 3i}{2})(2) = 0 \\
\end{align*}

Hence the second row becomes $0$.

So the system reduces to:

\begin{align*}
(1 + 5i)z + 2w &= 0 \\
w = -\frac{1 + 5i}{2}z \\
\end{align*}

Thus,

$$ker(T) = \left\{ z \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -\frac{1 + 5i}{2} \end{pmatrix} : z \in \mathbb{C} \right\}$$
