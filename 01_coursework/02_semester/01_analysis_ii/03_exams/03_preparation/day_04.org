#+begin_quote
Find the extrema of $f : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}$,

$$f(x, y, z) = xyz$$

subject to the constraint

$$x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 12.$$
#+end_quote

** Solution

\begin{align*}
\nabla f(x,y,z) &= \begin{pmatrix} yz \\ xz \\ xy \end{pmatrix} \\
\nabla g(x,y,z) &= \begin{pmatrix} 2x \\ 2y \\ 2z \end{pmatrix} \\
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
\nabla f &= \lambda \nabla g \\
\begin{pmatrix} yz \\ xz \\ xy \end{pmatrix} &=
\lambda \begin{pmatrix} 2x \\ 2y \\ 2z \end{pmatrix} \\
&\implies
\begin{cases}
yz = \lambda 2x \\
xz = \lambda 2y \\
xy = \lambda 2z \\
x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 12
\end{cases} \\
&\implies
\begin{cases}
xyz = \lambda 2x^2 = \lambda 2y^2 = \lambda 2z^2 \\
x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 12
\end{cases}
\end{align*}

*Case $\lambda = 0$.* Then $yz=xz=xy=0$, forcing at least two coordinates to be $0$, $f = 0$.

*Case $\lambda \neq 0$.* Then $x^2 = y^2 = z^2$. Constraint gives $3x^2 = 12$, so $|x| = |y| = |z| = 2$.

Hence, $f(x,y,z) = xyz = \pm8$.

- $\max(f) = 8$: $(2,2,2),\ (2,-2,-2),\ (-2,2,-2),\ (-2,-2,2)$

- $\min(f) = -8$: $(-2,-2,-2),\ (-2,2,2),\ (2,-2,2),\ (2,2,-2)$.

$$f(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2$$

subject to the constraints

$$x + y + z = 6, \qquad x - y = 2.$$

** Solution

\begin{align*}
c_1(x,y,z) &= x + y + z - 6 = 0 \\
c_2(x,y,z) &= x - y - 2 = 0 \\
\nabla f(x,y,z) &= \begin{pmatrix} 2x \\ 2y \\ 2z \end{pmatrix} \\
\nabla c_1(x,y,z) &= \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \\
\nabla c_2(x,y,z) &= \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \\
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
\begin{pmatrix} 2x \\ 2y \\ 2z \end{pmatrix} &=
\lambda \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} +
\mu \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \\
&\implies \begin{cases} 2x = \lambda + \mu \\ 2y = \lambda - \mu \\ 2z = \lambda \end{cases} \\
&\implies
\begin{cases}
x + y = 2z \\
x - y = \mu &\implies \mu = 2
\end{cases} \\
&\implies (\lambda + 2) + (\lambda - 2) + (\lambda) = 12 \implies \lambda = 4 \\
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
x &= 3 \\
y &= 1 \\
z &= 2
\end{align*}

Point $(3, 1, 2)^\top$, $f = 9 + 1 + 4 = 14$. The constraint set is a line in $\mathbb{R}^3$, so $f$ (squared distance from the origin) is unbounded above and $14$ is the global *minimum*. No maximum.

\nabla L(x, \lambda) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
2x_1 - 2 \lambda x_1 \\
4x_2 - 2 \lambda x_2
\end{pmatrix} \\

\begin{pmatrix}
2 - 2 \lambda & 0 \\
0 & 4 - 2 \lambda \\
\end{pmatrix}
\end{align*}

$\lambda = 2$: $x_1 = 0 \implies x_2 = \pm 1$
