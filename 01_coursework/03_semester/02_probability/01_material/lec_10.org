* Exact probabilities and upper bounds

Fix $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $p \in (0, 1)$, and $\varepsilon > 0$. For $i = 1, \dots, n$, let $X_i$ indicate failure of transmission $i$ in a model with independent transmissions and known failure probability $p$. Thus $X_i \sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)$; put

\[ \overline{X}_n = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i \]

For $p = 0.2$, $n = 200$, and $\varepsilon = 0.1$,

| method                     | $\mathbb{P}(\lvert \overline{X}_n - p \rvert \geq \varepsilon)$ |
|----------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------|
| exact binomial calculation | $0.000604$                                                      |
| Chebyshev                  | $\leq 0.08$                                                     |
| Hoeffding                  | $\leq 0.0367$                                                   |

An upper bound is a guarantee, not an estimate of the probability. A valid bound can be numerically loose because it is designed to cover every model satisfying its assumptions.

* Markov and Chebyshev inequalities

If $Y \geq 0$ almost surely, $\mathbb{E}[Y] < \infty$, and $a > 0$, then

\[ \mathbb{P}(Y \geq a) \leq \frac{\mathbb{E}[Y]}{a} \quad \text{(Markov's inequality)} \]

Indeed,

\[ Y \geq a \mathbf{1}_{\{Y \geq a\}} \]

and taking expectations proves the bound.

The assumptions do not permit a universally smaller bound. If $0 < \mu \leq a$ and

\[ \mathbb{P}(Y = a) = \frac{\mu}{a}, \qquad \mathbb{P}(Y = 0) = 1 - \frac{\mu}{a} \]

then $\mathbb{E}[Y] = \mu$ and equality holds in Markov's inequality at $a$.

If $Y$ has finite mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$, then for every $\varepsilon > 0$, applying Markov to $(Y - \mu)^2$ gives

\begin{align*}
\mathbb{P}(\lvert Y - \mu \rvert \geq \varepsilon) &= \mathbb{P}((Y - \mu)^2 \geq \varepsilon^2) \\
&\leq \frac{\mathbb{E}[(Y - \mu)^2]}{\varepsilon^2}
\end{align*}

Therefore

\[ \mathbb{P}(\lvert Y - \mu \rvert \geq \varepsilon) \leq \frac{\sigma^2}{\varepsilon^2} \quad \text{(Chebyshev's inequality)} \]

A bound larger than one remains true but is uninformative; it may be replaced by its minimum with one. Lecture 11 extends the exponential method to sub-Gaussian weighted sums and simultaneous bounds. Lectures 13--14 use these bounds to prove laws of large numbers, and Lecture 20 applies them to Monte Carlo error.

Let $n \in \mathbb{N}$, and let $X_1, \dots, X_n$ be independent with common mean $\mu$ and finite variance $\sigma^2$. For $\overline{X}_n = n^{-1} \sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i$,

\[ \mathbb{E}[\overline{X}_n] = \mu, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(\overline{X}_n) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n} \]

Hence

\[ \mathbb{P}(\lvert \overline{X}_n - \mu \rvert \geq \varepsilon) \leq \frac{\sigma^2}{n \varepsilon^2} \]

For Bernoulli variables, $\sigma^2 = p(1 - p) \leq 1/4$.

* The union bound

For $M \in \mathbb{N}$ and events $A_1, \dots, A_M$ on the same probability space,

\[ \mathbb{P}\left( \bigcup_{s=1}^{M} A_s \right) \leq \sum_{s=1}^{M} \mathbb{P}(A_s) \]

This follows from

\[ \mathbf{1}_{\bigcup_{s=1}^{M} A_s} \leq \sum_{s=1}^{M} \mathbf{1}_{A_s} \]

by taking expectations. Independence is not required. The bound counts the opportunities for at least one failure and can be loose when the events overlap strongly.

* The exponential method and Hoeffding's lemma

Let $Y$ be a real random variable, $c \in \mathbb{R}$, and $u > 0$ with $\mathbb{E}[e^{uY}] < \infty$. Applying Markov's inequality to $e^{uY}$ gives

\[ \mathbb{P}(Y \geq c) \leq e^{-uc} \mathbb{E}[e^{uY}] \]

The free parameter $u$ is chosen to minimize the resulting upper bound.

For $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$ with $a \leq b$, if $\mathbb{E}[Y] = 0$ and $a \leq Y \leq b$ almost surely, Hoeffding's lemma states that

\[ \mathbb{E}[e^{uY}] \leq \exp\left( \frac{u^2 (b - a)^2}{8} \right), \qquad u \in \mathbb{R} \]

#+begin_proof
Write $M_Y(u) = \mathbb{E}[e^{uY}] > 0$ and $\psi(u) = \log M_Y(u)$. Boundedness permits differentiation under the expectation on every compact $u$-interval, giving $M_Y'(u) = \mathbb{E}[Y e^{uY}]$ and $M_Y''(u) = \mathbb{E}[Y^2 e^{uY}]$. Hence

\[ \psi'(u) = \frac{M_Y'(u)}{M_Y(u)}, \qquad \psi''(u) = \frac{M_Y''(u)}{M_Y(u)} - \left( \frac{M_Y'(u)}{M_Y(u)} \right)^2 \]

Expanding the squares around the midpoint of $[a, b]$,

\begin{align*}
\psi''(u) &= \frac{\mathbb{E}[(Y - (a + b)/2)^2 e^{uY}]}{M_Y(u)} - \left( \frac{M_Y'(u)}{M_Y(u)} - \frac{a + b}{2} \right)^2 \\
&\leq \frac{\mathbb{E}[(Y - (a + b)/2)^2 e^{uY}]}{M_Y(u)} \leq \frac{(b - a)^2}{4}
\end{align*}

The last step uses $\lvert Y - (a + b)/2 \rvert \leq (b - a)/2$. Since $\psi(0) = \psi'(0) = 0$, Taylor's formula gives, for $u \geq 0$,

\[ \psi(u) = \int_0^u (u - v) \psi''(v) \, dv \leq \frac{u^2 (b - a)^2}{8} \]

Apply the same argument to $-Y \in [-b, -a]$ for $u < 0$, then exponentiate. This also covers $a = b$. $\blacksquare$
#+end_proof

* Hoeffding's inequality

Let $n \in \mathbb{N}$, let $X_1, \dots, X_n$ be independent, and let $a_i, b_i \in \mathbb{R}$ satisfy, for $i = 1, \dots, n$,

\[ a_i \leq X_i \leq b_i \quad \text{almost surely} \]

If $\sum_{i=1}^{n} (b_i - a_i)^2 > 0$, then for every $c > 0$,

\[ \mathbb{P}\left( \left\lvert \sum_{i=1}^{n} (X_i - \mathbb{E}[X_i]) \right\rvert \geq c \right) \leq 2 \exp\left( -\frac{2c^2}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} (b_i - a_i)^2} \right) \]

To see the main argument, let $Z_i = X_i - \mathbb{E}[X_i]$, whose range still has length $b_i - a_i$. For $u > 0$,

\begin{align*}
\mathbb{P}\left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} Z_i \geq c \right) &\leq e^{-uc} \mathbb{E}\left[ e^{u \sum_i Z_i} \right] \\
&= e^{-uc} \prod_{i=1}^{n} \mathbb{E}[e^{u Z_i}] \\
&\leq \exp\left( -uc + \frac{u^2}{8} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (b_i - a_i)^2 \right)
\end{align*}

The product step uses independence. Choosing $u = 4c / \sum_{i=1}^{n} (b_i - a_i)^2$ minimizes the exponent and gives the upper-tail bound. Apply the same argument to $-Z_i$ and use the union bound for the two-sided result. If all interval lengths are zero, the centered sum is zero almost surely and its tail probability is zero at every positive threshold.

If the variables have common mean $\mu$ and all lie in $[a, b]$ with $a < b$, define $\overline{X}_n = n^{-1} \sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i$. For $\varepsilon > 0$, set $c = n\varepsilon$ to obtain

\[ \mathbb{P}(\lvert \overline{X}_n - \mu \rvert \geq \varepsilon) \leq 2 \exp\left( -\frac{2 n \varepsilon^2}{(b - a)^2} \right) \]

Identical distributions are not required. Independence and boundedness are essential to this version.
