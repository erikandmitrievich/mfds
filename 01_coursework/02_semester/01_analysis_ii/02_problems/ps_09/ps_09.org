* Problem 1 :matrix:operator_norm:norm:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Consider $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ and prove the following claims made in class:

(a) If $\mathbb{R}^m$ and $\mathbb{R}^n$ are equipped with the sum norm, then the operator norm of $A$ is the column sum norm $\|A\|_1$.

(b) If $\mathbb{R}^m$ and $\mathbb{R}^n$ are equipped with the max norm, then the operator norm of $A$ is the row sum norm $\|A\|_\infty$.

(c) Find matrices $A$ such that
- $\|A\|_1 = \|A\|_\infty$,
- $\|A\|_1 > \|A\|_\infty$,
- $\|A\|_1 < \|A\|_\infty$.
#+end_quote

** Solution
*** (a)

*Claim.*

$$\sup_{\|x\|_1 = 1} \|Ax\|_1 = \max_{j=1, \ldots, n} \sum_{i=1}^{m} |a_{ij}|$$

*Proof.*

- $\le$

  Choose $x \in \mathbb{R}^n : \|x\|_1 = 1$ ($\sum_{j=1}^n |x_j| = 1$).

  \begin{align*}
  \|Ax\|_1 = \|\sum_{j=1}^n x_j a_{\cdot j}\|_1 \le \sum_{j=1}^n |x_{j}| \cdot \|a_{\cdot j}\|_1 &\le \sum_{j=1}^n |x_{j}| \cdot \max_j \|a_{\cdot j}\|_1 = \max_j \|a_{\cdot j}\|_1 \\
  \sup_{\|x\|_1 = 1} \|Ax\|_1 &\le \max_{j=1, \ldots, n} \sum_{i=1}^{m} |a_{ij}| \\
  \end{align*}

- $\ge$

  Let $j^{*} := \arg \max_{j=1, \ldots, n} \sum_{i=1}^m |a_{ij}|$. Choose $x = e_{j^{*}}$. Then $\|e_{j^{*}}\|_1 = 1$ and

  \begin{align*}
  \|Ae_{j^{*}}\|_1 = \|a_{\cdot j^{*}}\|_1 &= \sum_{i=1}^m |a_{ij^{*}}| = \max_{j=1, \ldots, n} \sum_{i=1}^{m} |a_{ij}| \\
  \sup_{\|x\|_1 = 1} \|Ax\|_1 &\ge \max_{j=1, \ldots, n} \sum_{i=1}^{m} |a_{ij}| \\
  \end{align*}

Thus,

$$\sup_{\|x\|_1 = 1} \|Ax\|_1 = \max_{j=1, \ldots, n} \sum_{i=1}^{m} |a_{ij}|$$

*** (b)

*Claim.*

$$\sup_{\|x\|_{\infty} = 1} \|Ax\|_{\infty} = \max_{i=1, \ldots, m} \sum_{j = 1}^n |a_{ij}|$$

*Proof.*

- $\le$

  Choose $x \in \mathbb{R}^n : \|x\|_{\infty} = 1$.

  \begin{align*}
  \|Ax\|_{\infty} = \max_{i = 1, \ldots, m} \left|\sum_{j}^n a_{ij} x_{j}\right| &\le \max_{i = 1, \ldots, m} \sum_{j}^n |a_{ij}| \cdot \|x\|_{\infty} = \max_{i = 1, \ldots, m} \sum_{j}^n |a_{ij}| \\
  \|Ax\|_{\infty} &\le \max_{i = 1, \ldots, m} \sum_{j}^n |a_{ij}| \\
  \end{align*}

- $\ge$

  Let $i^{*} = \arg \max_{i = 1, \ldots, m} \sum_{j=1}^n |a_{ij}|$. Choose $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$, by

  $$x_j = \mathrm{sgn}(a_{i^{*}j}), j = 1, \ldots, n$$

  with the convention $\mathrm{sgn}(0) = 1$. Then, $|x_j| \le 1$ for all $j$ and $|x_j| = 1$ whenever $a_{i^{*}j} \neq 0$, so $\|x\|_{\infty} = 1$.

  The $i^{*}$ th component of $Ax$ is:

  $$(Ax)_{i^{*}} = \sum_{j=0}^{n} a_{i^{*}j}x_{j} = \sum_{j=0}^{n} a_{i^{*}j} \mathrm{sgn}(a_{i^{*}j})=\sum_{j=0}^{n}|a_{i^{*}j}|$$

  Therefore,

  \begin{align*}
  \|Ax\|{\infty} = \max_{i=1, \ldots, m} \|(Ax)i\| &\ge |(Ax){i^{}}| = \sum_{j=1}^n |a_{i^{}j}| = \max_{i=1, \ldots, m} \sum_{j=1}^n |a_{ij}| \\
  \|Ax\|_{\infty} &\ge \max_{i = 1, \ldots, m} \sum_{j}^n |a_{ij}| \\
  \end{align*}

Thus,

$$\sup_{\|x\|_{\infty} = 1} \|Ax\|_{\infty} = \max_{i=1, \ldots, m} \sum_{j = 1}^n |a_{ij}|$$

*** (c)

- $\|A\|_1 = \|A\|_\infty$: $A = I_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}. \quad \|A\|_1 = 1 = \|A\|_\infty$.

- $\|A\|_1 > \|A\|_\infty$: $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}. \quad \|A\|_1 = 2, \|A\|_\infty = 1$.

- $\|A\|_1 < \|A\|_\infty$: $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}. \quad \|A\|_1 = 1, \|A\|_\infty = 2$.

*** (b)

*Proposition.* Let $g \in \mathcal{L}(B)$ be such that $\mathrm{id}_B - g$ is invertible in $\mathcal{L}(B)$. Then for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$,

$$\sum_{k=0}^n g^k = (\mathrm{id}_B - g^{n+1})(\mathrm{id}_B - g)^{-1}.$$

*Base case* ($n = 0$).

\begin{align*}
\sum_{k=0}^0 g^k = \mathrm{id}_B = (\mathrm{id}_B - g)(\mathrm{id}_B - g)^{-1} = (\mathrm{id}_B - g^{0+1})(\mathrm{id}_B - g)^{-1}.
\end{align*}

*Inductive step.* Assume $\sum_{k=0}^n g^k = (\mathrm{id}_B - g^{n+1})(\mathrm{id}_B - g)^{-1}$. Then

\begin{align*}
\sum_{k=0}^{n+1} g^k &= \sum_{k=0}^{n} g^k + g^{n+1} \\
                    &= (\mathrm{id}_B - g^{n+1})(\mathrm{id}_B - g)^{-1} + g^{n+1}(\mathrm{id}_B - g)(\mathrm{id}_B - g)^{-1} \\
                    &= \left((\mathrm{id}_B - g^{n+1}) + g^{n+1}(\mathrm{id}_B - g)\right)(\mathrm{id}_B - g)^{-1} \\
                    &= (\mathrm{id}_B - g^{n+1} + g^{n+1} - g^{n+2})(\mathrm{id}_B - g)^{-1} \\
                    &= (\mathrm{id}_B - g^{(n+1)+1})(\mathrm{id}_B - g)^{-1}.
\end{align*}

*Conclusion.* By induction, the identity holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}_0$. $\square$

* Problem 3 :matrix:inverse:von_neumann_series:

#+begin_quote
Consider the matrix

\begin{align*}
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac{1}{2} & 0 & 0 \\
0 & \frac{1}{3} & 0 \\
0 & 0 & \frac{1}{4} \\
\end{pmatrix}
\end{align*}

(a) Determine the inverse of $A$ by computing the associated von Neumann series by hand.

(b) Implement the von Neumann iteration and compute the inverse of $B$ in Julia:

$$B = \begin{pmatrix} 1.20 & 0.05 & 0 & 0 & 0.02 \\ 0.03 & 0.90 & 0.04 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0.02 & 1.10 & -0.03 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0.05 & 0.85 & 0.04 \\ 0.01 & 0 & 0 & 0.02 & 1.15 \end{pmatrix}$$

(c) Use "Rational{BigInt}" in Julia and check that "inv(B)" and the von Neumann series computed till the first $n = 150$ terms in Rational{BigInt} deviate by at most $10^{-100}$ in each entry.
#+end_quote

** Solution
*** (a)

Since $I - A = \mathrm{diag}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{2}{3}, \tfrac{3}{4}\right)$ and $\|I - A\| = \tfrac{3}{4} < 1$, the von Neumann series converges.

\begin{align*}
A^{-1} &= \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (I - A)^k \\
       &= \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \mathrm{diag}\!\left(\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^k,\, \left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^k,\, \left(\tfrac{3}{4}\right)^k\right) \\
       &= \mathrm{diag}\!\left(\sum_{k=0}^\infty \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^k,\; \sum_{k=0}^\infty \left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^k,\; \sum_{k=0}^\infty \left(\tfrac{3}{4}\right)^k\right) \\
       &= \mathrm{diag}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{1 - 1/2},\, \tfrac{1}{1 - 2/3},\, \tfrac{1}{1 - 3/4}\right) \\
       &= \mathrm{diag}(2,\, 3,\, 4) \\
       &= \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix} \\
\end{align*}

* Problem 4 :normed_space:linear_map:continuity:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Consider the space

$$C([0, 1]) := \{f : [0, 1] \to \mathbb{R} : f \text{ continuous}\}$$

(a) Prove that $C([0, 1])$ is a normed, linear vector space with norm
$$\|f\| := \int_0^1 |f(x)|\, dx$$

(You may assume that this is a norm...)

(b) Show that the map $S : C([0, 1]) \to \mathbb{R}$,

$$S(f) = f(0)$$

is linear but not continuous.

(c) Is there an alternative norm on $C([0, 1])$ such that $S$ is continuous?
#+end_quote

** Solution
*** (a)

Let $F([0,1]) := \{f : [0,1] \rightarrow \mathbb{R}\}$, which is a real vector space under pointwise operations

$$(f+g)(x) := f(x) + g(x), \quad (\alpha f)(x) = \alpha f(x)$$

The vector space axioms hold because they hold pointwise in $\mathbb{R}$. To show $C([0,1])$ is a vector space, its sufficient to prove its linear subspace of $F([0,1])$.

#+begin_proof
1. *Zero function.* The map $\mathbf{0}(x) = 0$ satisfies $|\mathbf{0}(x) - \mathbf{0}(x_0)| = 0 < \epsilon$ for all $\epsilon > 0$, hence $\mathbf{0} \in C([0,1])$.

2. *Closure under addition.* Let $f, g \in C([0,1])$, fix $x_0 \in [0,1]$ and $\epsilon > 0$. By continuity, $\exists \delta_1, \delta_2 > 0$ such that

   $$|x - x_0| < \delta_1 \Rightarrow |f(x) - f(x_0)| < \epsilon/2, \quad |x - x_0| < \delta_2 \Rightarrow |g(x) - g(x_0)| < \epsilon/2.$$

   For $\delta := \min(\delta_1, \delta_2)$ and $|x - x_0| < \delta$,

   $$|(f+g)(x) - (f+g)(x_0)| \le |f(x) - f(x_0)| + |g(x) - g(x_0)| < \epsilon.$$

3. *Closure under scalar multiplication.* Let $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$, $f \in C([0,1])$. If $\alpha = 0$, then $\alpha f = \mathbf{0}$, covered by (i). If $\alpha \neq 0$, fix $x_0$ and $\epsilon > 0$. By continuity of $f$, $\exists \delta > 0$ such that $|x - x_0| < \delta \Rightarrow |f(x) - f(x_0)| < \epsilon / |\alpha|$. Then

   $$|\alpha f(x) - \alpha f(x_0)| = |\alpha| \, |f(x) - f(x_0)| < \epsilon.$$
#+end_proof

Hence $C([0,1])$ is a linear subspace of $F([0,1])$, hence a real vector space. The norm axioms for $\|f\| = \int_0^1 |f(x)| \, dx$ are assumed.
