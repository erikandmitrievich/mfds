#+begin_quote
Consider the function $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$,

\[ f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 + 2x + 4y \]

(a) Determine all local extrema of $f$.

(b) Plot $f$ in Julia by a heatmap to check your calculations in Part (a).
#+end_quote

** Solution

*** (a)

\begin{align*}
f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 + 2x + 4y \\
\end{align*}


\begin{align*}
\nabla f(x, y) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
2x + 2 \\
2y + 4
\end{pmatrix} \\
&\implies
\begin{cases}
2x + 2 = 0 &\implies x = -1 \\
2y + 4 = 0 &\implies y = -2
\end{cases} \\
&\implies (-1,\ -2) \text{ is a critical point}
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
H_f(x,y) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 0 \\
0 & 2
\end{pmatrix}
\end{align*}

The eigenvalues ($\lambda_1 = 2,\ \lambda_2 = 2$) are strictly positive $\implies$ Hessian is positive definite and thus the only critical point $(-1, -2)$ is a local minimum.

(b) Visualize $f$ and $\nabla f$ in Julia for $n = 1$.
#+end_quote

** Solution

*** (a)

\begin{align*}
\nabla f(x, y) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
2x \\
-2y
\end{pmatrix} \\
&\implies (0, 0) \text{ is a critical point}
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
H_f(x,y) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 0 \\
0 & -2 \\
\end{pmatrix}
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
\begin{pmatrix} x & y \end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 0 \\
0 & -2 \\
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} &=
\begin{pmatrix} 2x & -2y \end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} \\
&= 2x^2 - 2y^2 \\
&\implies \text{indefinite}
\end{align*}

Thus, $(0,0)$ is a saddle point.

#+begin_quote
Consider $f: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ given by

\[
f(x, y) = \sqrt{1 + x^2 + y^2}.
\]

Determine all critical points and classify them into local minima, maxima, and saddle points.
#+end_quote

** Solution

\begin{align*}
\nabla f(x, y) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{1 + x^2 + y^2}} \\
\dfrac{y}{\sqrt{1 + x^2 + y^2}}
\end{pmatrix} \\
&\implies x,y = 0
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
H_f(x, y) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{1 + y^2}{(1 + x^2 + y^2)^{3/2}} & \dfrac{-xy}{(1 + x^2 + y^2)^{3/2}} \\
\dfrac{-xy}{(1 + x^2 + y^2)^{3/2}} & \dfrac{1 + x^2}{(1 + x^2 + y^2)^{3/2}}
\end{pmatrix} \\
H_f(0, 0) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
0 & 1
\end{pmatrix}
\end{align*}

The eigenvalues ($\lambda_1, \lambda_2 = 1$) are positive $\implies$ $H_f(x, y)$ is strictly positive $\implies$ $(0,0)$ is local minimum.

#+begin_quote
Consider the function $f: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ given by

\[
f(x, y) = \sin x + \cos y.
\]

(a) Determine all critical points and classify them as local minima, maxima, and saddle points.

(b) Show that $\nabla f$ is $L$ -Lipschitz continuous.

(c) Compute all critical points within $[0, 2\pi] \times [-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{3}{2}\pi]$ in Julia via gradient descent by selecting suitable initial vectors $(x_0, y_0)$.
#+end_quote

** Solution

*** (a)

\begin{align*}
\nabla f(x, y) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos(x) \\
-\sin(y)
\end{pmatrix} \\
&\implies
\begin{cases}
\cos(x) = 0 &\implies x = \{ \frac{1}{2}\pi + n\pi : n \in \mathbb{N} \} \\
-\sin(y) = 0 &\implies y = \{ m\pi : m \in \mathbb{N} \}
\end{cases}
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
H_f(x, y) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
-\sin(x) & 0 \\
0 & -\cos(y)
\end{pmatrix} \\
H_f(\frac{1}{2}\pi + n\pi, m\pi) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
-\sin(\frac{1}{2}\pi + n\pi) & 0 \\
0 & -\cos(m\pi)
\end{pmatrix} \\
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
(-1)^{n+1} & 0 \\
0 & (-1)^{m+1}
\end{pmatrix}
\end{align*}

- $n$ odd, $m$ odd: (positive eigenvalues) $H_f$ is positive definite $\implies$ strict local minimum

- $n$ even, $m$ even: (negative eigenvalues) $H_f$ is negative definite $\implies$ strict local maximum

- $n$ odd, $m$ even: (mixed sign eigenvalues) $H_f$ is indefinite $\implies$ saddle point

- $n$ even, $m$ odd: (mixed sign eigenvalues) $H_f$ is indefinite $\implies$ saddle point

**** Grasped

1. Worked with trigonometric functions in terms of differentiation and critical points classification.

*** (b)

\begin{align*}
\nabla f(x,y) - \nabla f(\tilde{x}, \tilde{y}) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos(x) - \cos(\tilde{x}) \\
-\sin(y) + \sin(\tilde{y}) \\
\end{pmatrix}
\end{align*}

By the mean value theorem applied to $\sin$ and $\cos$ on $\mathbb{R}$, together with $|\sin'| = |\cos| \leq 1$ and $|\cos'| = |\sin| \leq 1$:

\begin{align*}
|\cos(x) - \cos(\tilde{x})| &\leq |x - \tilde{x}| \\
|\sin(y) - \sin(\tilde{y})| &\leq |y - \tilde{y}|
\end{align*}

Hence

\begin{align*}
\|\nabla f(x,y) - \nabla f(\tilde{x}, \tilde{y})\|^2
&= (\cos(x) - \cos(\tilde{x}))^2 + (\sin(y) - \sin(\tilde{y}))^2 \\
&\leq (x - \tilde{x})^2 + (y - \tilde{y})^2 \\
&= \|(x,y) - (\tilde{x}, \tilde{y})\|^2.
\end{align*}

Therefore $\nabla f$ is $L$ -Lipschitz with $L = 1$. $\blacksquare$
