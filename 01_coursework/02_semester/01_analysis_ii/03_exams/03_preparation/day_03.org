#+begin_quote
Let $f : (-\frac{1}{2}, \infty) \times (-\frac{1}{2}, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}$ be defined by

\[ f(x, y) = \log(1 + x + y) \]

(a) Determine the Taylor polynomial of order 3 of $f$ at $(0, 0)$.
(b) Use your result to approximate $f(0.1, 0.1)$.
(c) Plot heatmaps of $f$, of the Taylor polynomial of order 3, and of the difference of the two in $[0, 1]^2$.
#+end_quote

** Solution

*** (a)

$$f(x) = \sum_{|\alpha| \le k} \dfrac{\partial^{\alpha} f(x_0)}{\alpha!}(x - x_0)^{\alpha} + o(\|x - x_0\|^k) \quad (x \rightarrow x_0)$$

\begin{align*}
\frac{d}{dx} \log_a(x) &= \frac{1}{x \ln(a)} \\
\frac{d}{dx} \log_a(f(x)) &= \frac{1}{f(x) \ln(a)}f'(x) \\
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
|\alpha| &= \alpha_1 + \alpha_2 \\
\alpha! &= \alpha_1! \alpha_2! \\
x^{\alpha} &= x_1^{\alpha_1} x_2^{\alpha_2} \qquad (x = (x,y),\ x^{\alpha} = x^{\alpha_1}y^{\alpha_2}) \\
\partial^{\alpha} f &= \dfrac{\partial^{|\alpha|} f}{\partial x^{\alpha_1} \partial y^{\alpha_2}}
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
\partial^{|\alpha| = 1} f &= \partial_1 f,\ \partial_2 f = (1 + x + y)^{-1} \\
\partial^{|\alpha| = 2} f &= \partial_1^2 f,\ \partial_1 \partial_2 f,\ \partial_2^2 f = -(1 + x + y)^{-2} \\
\partial^{|\alpha| = 3} f &= \partial_1^3 f,\ \partial_1^2 \partial_2 f,\ \partial_1 \partial_2^2 f,\ \partial_2^3 f = 2(1 + x + y)^{-3} \\
\end{align*}

#+begin_quote
Let $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ and $g : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be twice continuously differentiable such that $f(x, y) = g(x + y)$.

(a) Show that the Taylor polynomial of $g$ of order 1 centered at 0 and evaluated at $t = x + y$ coincides with the local (affine) linear approximation of $f$ in $(0, 0)$.

(b) The Hessian matrix of $f$ is

$$Hf(x, y) = \begin{pmatrix} \partial_x \partial_x f(x, y) & \partial_x \partial_y f(x, y) \\ \partial_y \partial_x f(x, y) & \partial_y \partial_y f(x, y) \end{pmatrix}$$

Consider $g(t) = e^t$ and show that the Taylor polynomial of $g$ of order 2 centered at 0 and evaluated at $t = x + y$ coincides with

$$F(x, y) = f(0, 0) + \operatorname{grad} f(0, 0) \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} + \frac{1}{2} (x, y) \, Hf(0, 0) \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}$$
#+end_quote

** Solution

*** (a)

Skip, useless for the exam.

*** (b)

$$f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \dfrac{f^n(x)}{n!}(x - x_0)^n$$

\begin{align*}
f(x,y) &= e^{x+y} \\
g(t) &= e^t \qquad (t = x + y) \\
T_2(t) &= g(0) + g'(0)t + \frac{g''(0)}{2}t^2 \\
       &= 1 + t + \frac{t^2}{2} \\
T_2(x,y) &= 1 + (x+y) + \frac{(x+y)^2}{2} \\
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
f(x,y) &= e^{x + y} \\
\nabla f(x,y) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{x + y} \\
e^{x + y}
\end{pmatrix} \\
H_f(x,y) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{x + y} & e^{x + y} \\
e^{x + y} & e^{x + y}
\end{pmatrix} \\
f(0,0) &= 1 \\
\nabla f(0,0) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
1 \\
1
\end{pmatrix} \\
H_f(0,0) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 \\
1 & 1
\end{pmatrix} \\
\end{align*}
\begin{align*}
F(x, y) &= f(0, 0) + \operatorname{grad} f(0, 0) \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} + \frac{1}{2} (x, y) \, Hf(0, 0) \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} \\
&= 1 +
\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} +
\frac{1}{2} (x, y) \,
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 \\
1 & 1
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} \\
&= 1 + x + y + \frac{1}{2}(x^2+xy + xy + y^2) \\
&= 1 + x + y + \frac{(x + y)^2}{2}
\end{align*}

Hence, $T_2(t) = F(x,y)$.

#+begin_quote
Consider the curve $f : [0, 1] \to \mathbb{R}^2$ given by

$$f(t) = \begin{pmatrix} t \\ \frac{2}{3} t^{3/2} \end{pmatrix}$$

Determine the length of $f$.
#+end_quote

** Solution

$$l(\gamma) = \int_0^1 \|\gamma'(t)\|dt$$

$$\gamma'(t) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \sqrt{t} \end{pmatrix}$$

\begin{align*}
l(\gamma) &= \int_0^1 \sqrt{(1)^2 + (\sqrt{t})^2} dt \\
          &= \int_0^1 \sqrt{1 + t} dt \\
          &= \Big[ \frac{2}{3}(1 + t)^{3/2} \Big]_0^1 \\
          &= (\frac{2}{3}(1 + 1)^{3/2}) - (\frac{2}{3}(1 + 0)^{3/2}) \\
          &= \frac{2}{3}\bigl(2\sqrt{2} - 1\bigr).
\end{align*}

#+begin_quote
Let $V$ and $W$ be normed spaces and let $f_1, \ldots, f_n : V \to \mathbb{R}$ be convex. Prove that the following functions are also convex.

(a) $\sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i f_i$ for $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n \geq 0$.
(b) $g : V \to \mathbb{R}$ given by $g(x) = \max_{i=1,\ldots,n} f_i(x)$.
(c) If $A : W \to V$ is linear and $b \in V$, then $g : W \to \mathbb{R}$ given by

\[ g(x) = f_1(Ax - b) \]

is also convex.
#+end_quote

** Solution

#+begin_quote
A function $f : \mathbb{R} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is called a convex iff $\forall x, y \in \mathbb{R}$ and $t \in [0,1]$,

$$f(tx + (1-t)y) \le tf(x) + (1-t)f(y)$$
#+end_quote

*** (a)

Let $h = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i f_i$ for $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n \ge 0$.

By convexity of each $f_i$:

$$f_i(tx + (1 - t)y) \le tf_i(x) + (1 - t)f_i(y)$$

Since $\alpha_i \ge 0$, multiplying preserves the inequality:

\begin{align*}
\sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i f_i(tx + (1 - t)y) &\le \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i t f_i(x) + \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i (1 - t) f_i(y) \\
\sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i f_i(tx + (1 - t)y) &\le t \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i f_i(x) + (1 - t) \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i f_i(y) \\
h(tx + (1 - t)y) &\le t h(x) + (1 - t) h(y) \\
\end{align*}

    n = length(f(0))
