* Definition:  Idealized floating point numbers
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: def:idealized-fp-numbers
:LECTURE_REF: 3.3.1
:END:

For a positive integer $m$, the idealized floating point numbers are

\[ \mathbb{R}^{[m]} \coloneqq \bigcup_{\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}} \{ \pm(1 + k2^{-m})2^{\alpha} \mid k = 0, 1, \dots, 2^m - 1 \} \cup \{0\} \]

The idealized machine precision is

\[ \mathrm{eps}^{[m]} \coloneqq 2^{-m} \]

* Lemma:       Relative error of idealized rounding
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: lem:idealized-rounding-error
:LECTURE_REF: 3.3.2
:END:

For every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, the mapping $\mathrm{fl}^{[m]}\colon \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^{[m]}$ satisfies

\[ \lvert \mathrm{fl}^{[m]}(x) - x \rvert \leq \mathrm{eps}^{[m]} \lvert x \rvert \tag{3.3} \]

#+begin_proof
Consider $x > 0$. There is $\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that $x \in [2^{\alpha}, 2^{\alpha+1})$. The definition of $\mathrm{fl}^{[m]}$ yields

\[ \lvert \mathrm{fl}^{[m]}(x) - x \rvert \leq \lvert 2^{\alpha-m} \rvert = 2^{-m}2^{\alpha} \leq \mathrm{eps}^{[m]} \lvert x \rvert \]

The inequality also holds for $x = 0$ and for $x < 0$ we derive

\[ \lvert \mathrm{fl}^{[m]}(x) - x \rvert = \lvert -\mathrm{fl}^{[m]}(x) + x \rvert = \lvert \mathrm{fl}^{[m]}(-x) - (-x) \rvert \]

so that the estimate for $-x > 0$ applies. $\blacksquare$
#+end_proof

* Theorem:     Fundamental Theorem of Computational Precision
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: thm:fundamental-computational-precision
:LECTURE_REF: 3.3.3
:END:

For $x \in \mathbb{R}$, there is $\lvert \varepsilon \rvert \leq \mathrm{eps}$ such that

\[ \mathrm{fl}^{(\mathrm{eps})}(x) = x(1 + \varepsilon) \tag{3.10} \]

For $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^{(\mathrm{eps})}$, there is $\lvert \varepsilon \rvert \leq \mathrm{eps}$ such that

\[ x * y = (x \star y)(1 + \varepsilon) \tag{3.11} \]

#+begin_proof
We may assume $x \neq 0$ and define $\varepsilon = \frac{\mathrm{fl}^{(\mathrm{eps})}(x) - x}{x}$. Then (3.10) holds and according to the axiom of computation we have

\[ \lvert \varepsilon \rvert = \left\lvert \frac{\mathrm{fl}^{(\mathrm{eps})}(x) - x}{x} \right\rvert \leq \mathrm{eps} \]

The claim (3.11) follows analogously. $\blacksquare$
#+end_proof

* Definition:  Floating point numbers
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: def:fp-numbers
:LECTURE_REF: 3.3.5

The floating point numbers (for the 64-bit IEEE standard) are

\[ \mathbb{R}_{64} \coloneqq \bigcup_{\alpha=-1022}^{1023} \{ \pm(1, a_1 a_2 \dots a_{52})_2 \cdot 2^{\alpha} \mid a_k \in \{0, 1\} \} \]

and we define

\[ R_{64} \coloneqq \max(\mathbb{R}_{64}), \qquad r_{64} \coloneqq \min(\{ x \in \mathbb{R}_{64} \mid x > 0 \}) \]

* Theorem:     Truncation of idealized floating point numbers
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: thm:truncation-idealized-fp
:LECTURE_REF: 3.3.6
:END:

The set $\mathbb{R}_{64}$ is

\[ \mathbb{R}_{64} = \bigcup_{\alpha=-1022}^{1023} \{ \pm(1 + k2^{-52})2^{\alpha} \mid k = 0, 1, \dots, 2^{52} - 1 \} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{[52]} \tag{3.13} \]

so that it is a union of equally spaced numbers with gaps $2^{\alpha-52}$.

#+begin_proof
The binary representation in (3.12) yields

\[ (1, a_1 a_2 \dots a_{52})_2 = 1 + \sum_{k=1}^{52} a_k 2^{-k} \]

and /Corollary 2.2.20/ implies

\[ \{ 1 + k\,2^{-52} \mid k = 0, 1, \dots, 2^{52} - 1 \} = \left\{ 1 + \sum_{j=1}^{52} a_j 2^{-j} \mid a_j \in \{0, 1\} \right\} \]

This concludes the proof. $\blacksquare$
#+end_proof

* Lemma:       Largest and smallest positive floating point numbers
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: lem:largest-smallest-positive-fp
:LECTURE_REF: 3.3.7
:END:

The largest and smallest positive floating point numbers are

\[ R_{64} = 2^{1024}(1 - 2^{-53}) > 10^{308}, \qquad r_{64} = 2^{-1022} < 3 \cdot 10^{-308} \]

#+begin_proof
The claim $r_{64} = 2^{-1022}$ is obvious. For $R_{64}$, we observe

\begin{align*}
R_{64} &= (1 + (2^{52} - 1)2^{-52})2^{1023} \\
       &= (2 - 2^{-52})2^{1023} \\
       &= (1 - 2^{-53})2^{1024}
\end{align*}

$\blacksquare$
#+end_proof

* Theorem:     Precision of computer arithmetics
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: thm:precision-computer-arithmetics-64
:LECTURE_REF: 3.3.11
:END:

For all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}_{64}$ satisfying $\lvert x \star y \rvert \in [r_{64}, R_{64}]$, we have

\[ \left\lvert \frac{x * y - (x \star y)}{x \star y} \right\rvert \leq \mathrm{eps}_{64} \tag{3.16} \]

#+begin_proof
The assertion follows directly from (3.15) and (3.14). $\blacksquare$
#+end_proof

:LECTURE_REF: 3.3.14

\[ \mathrm{Float}\,64 = \mathbb{R}_{64} \cup \mathbb{R}_{64,\mathrm{sub}} \cup \{ \pm 0, \pm \mathrm{Inf}, \mathrm{NaN} \} \]
