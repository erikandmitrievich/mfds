*** Annotated Rewriting

*Theorem (Dimension Theorem for Linear Maps).*

Let $V$ and $W$ be finite-dimensional $\mathbb{F}$ vector spaces, and let $f : V \rightarrow W$ be linear. Then

$$dim(V) = dim(im(f)) + dim(ker(f))$$

*Proof.*

We choose a basis $\left\{ b_1, \ldots, b_k \right\}$ for the subspace $ker(f) \subseteq V$.

/define basis of a kernel/

Since the vectors $b_i$ are linearly independent, /Corollary III.1.48(ii)/ shows they can be extended to a basis $\left\{ b_1, \ldots, b_n \right\}$ of the vector space $V$.

/using Corollary to state the basis set can be extended (with linear independent vectors) to span the whole vector space $V$/

We claim that the vectors $f(b_{k+1}), \ldots, f(b_{n}) \in W$ form a basis of the subspace $im(f) \in W$, which of course proves the theorem.

/outlining the whole point of the proof, to demonstrate $\left\{ f(b_{k+1}), \ldots, f(b_n) \right\}$ (extended part of initial kernel basis) is basis of image under $f$/

First, /Proposition III.2.11/ tells us that vectors $f(b_1), \ldots, f(b_n)$ form a generating system of $im(f)$.

/using proposition to state $\left\{ f(b_1), \ldots, f(b_n) \right\}$ spans the $im(f)$, foundation/

By construction, however, we have $f(b_1) = \ldots = f(b_k) = 0$, so $f(b_{k+1}), \ldots, f(b_n)$ also form a generating system.

/outline that only $\left\{ f(b_{k+1}), \ldots, f(b_n) \right\}$ actually spans the $im(f)$ (as linearly mapped vectors of kernel result in zero - no contribution)/

On the other hand, assume that $\lambda_{k+1}, \ldots, \lambda_n \in \mathbb{F}$ are scalars such that $\lambda_{k+1}f(b_{k+1}) + \ldots + \lambda_{n}f(b_n) = 0$ holds.

/assume existence of scalars $\lambda_{k+1}, \ldots, \lambda_{n} \in \mathbb{F}$ such that, $\lambda_{k+1}f(b_{k+1}) + \ldots + \lambda_{n}f(b_n) = 0$/

Then we can write the left hand side as $f(\lambda_{k+1}b_{k+1} + \ldots + \lambda_n b_n)$, hence, $\lambda_{k+1}b_{k+1} + \ldots + \lambda_n b_n \in ker(f)$.

/using linearity of $f$ to rewrite the scaled vector sums into the form of $f(\lambda_{k+1}b_{k+1} + \ldots + \lambda_n b_n)$, and hence, as scaled vector sum equals to zero, it is within the kernel of $f$ (by definition of a kernel $ker(T) = \left\{ T(v) = 0 : v \in V \right\}$)/

By construction, the vectors $b_1, \ldots, b_k$ form a basis of this subspace, so we find scalars $\mu_1, \ldots, \mu_k \in \mathbb{F}$ such that

$$\lambda_{k+1}b_{k+1} + \ldots + \lambda_nb_n = \mu_1b_1 + \ldots + \mu_kb_k$$

/as scaled vector sum (constructed with $b_{k+1}, \ldots, b_{n}$) is in kernel, it can be written as scaled vector sum of $b_1, \ldots, b_k$, (there must exist scalars such that) $\lambda_{k+1}b_{k+1} + \ldots + \lambda_nb_n = \mu_1b_1 + \ldots + \mu_kb_k$ /

But this means precisely that

$$\mu_1b_1 + \ldots + \mu_kb_k - \lambda_{k+1}b_{k+1} - \ldots - \lambda_nb_n = 0$$

/rewriting the equation to further invoke property of linear independence of $b_1, \ldots, b_n$/

and by linear independence of $b_1, \ldots, b_n$ this is possible only if

$$\mu_1 = \ldots = \mu_k = \lambda_{k+1} = \ldots = \lambda_n = 0$$

/as $b_1, \ldots, b_n$ are linearly independent (as are basis of vector space $V$) the only way the total sum of scaled vectors can be equal to zero iff all scalars equal to zero, hence, as $f(b_{k+1}), \dots, f(b_n)$ are generating system of $im(f)$ and also are linear independent, they are a basis of $im(f)$/

/to sum up, as $\left\{ b_1, \ldots, b_n \right\}$ is basis of vector space, let $\left\{ b_{1}, \ldots, b_k \right\}$ be basis of $ker(f)$ ($k \leq n$). the extended part obviously spans $im(f)$, and now it only needs to prove its linear independence (by using linearity and scalars operations). as extended part spans $im(f)$ and is linear independent, it is a basis of $im(f)$, which implies $dim(V) = dim(ker(f)) + dim(im(f))$/

*** Personal Review

Let $V$ and $W$ be a finite dimensional $F$ vector spaces.

Let $f : V \rightarrow W$ be linear map.

Let $\left\{ b_1, \ldots, b_k \right\}$ be a basis of $ker(f) \subseteq V$.

Proving kernel basis can be extended to $n$ values (keep in mind $b_i$ are all linearly independent), with $\left\{ f(b_{k+1}), \ldots, f(b_n) \right\}$ being a basis of $im(f) \subseteq W$.

Claim $\left\{ f(b_1), \ldots, f(b_n) \right\}$ form a basis of $im(f)$.

Note $f(b_1) = \ldots = f(b_k) = 0$ (as they are kernel), while only $\left\{ b_{k+1}, \ldots, b_n \right\}$ are "contributing" to the span of $im(f)$.

Assume $\exists \, \lambda_{k+1}, \ldots, \lambda_n \in \mathbb{F}$ (scalars) such that $\lambda_{k+1}f(b_{k+1}) + \ldots + \lambda_nf(b_n) = 0$.

Hence, (rewriting) $f(\lambda_{k+1}b_{k+1} + \ldots + \lambda_nb_n) = 0$.

Thus, $\lambda_{k+1}b_{k+1} + \ldots + \lambda_nb_n \in ker(f)$ (as their sum is zero).

Since $\left\{ b_1, \ldots, b_k \right\}$ are basis, there must exist scalars $\mu_1, \ldots, \mu_k \in \mathbb{F}$ such that $\lambda_{k+1}b_{k+1} + \ldots + \lambda_nb_n = \mu_1b_1 + \ldots + \mu_kb_k$

Hence, $\mu_1b_1 + \ldots + \mu_kb_k - \lambda_{k+1}b_{k+1} - \ldots - \lambda_nb_n = 0$.

And by linear independence of $b_1, \ldots, b_n$, this is possible iff $\mu_1 = \ldots = \mu_k = \lambda_{k+1} = \ldots = \lambda_n = 0$.

*** Exam Style

- *Theorem:*

  For a finite dimensional vector space $V$ and a linear map $f : V \rightarrow W$, $dim(V) = dim(ker(f)) + dim(im(f))$.

- *Proof:*

  Let $dim(V) = n$ and $dim(ker(f)) = k$.

  1. Basis Extension

     Choose a basis $\left\{ b_1, \ldots, b_k \right\}$ for $ker(f)$. Extend it to basis $\left\{ b_1, \ldots, b_n \right\}$ for $V$.

  2. Claim

     The set $S = \left\{ f(b_{k+1}), \ldots, f(b_n) \right\}$ is basis for $im(f)$.

  3. Proof of Span

     Let $y \in im(f)$, then $\exists x \in V : y = f(x)$. Since $\left\{ b_1, \dots, b_n \right\}$ is a basis for $V$, we can write $x = \sum_{i = 1}^n c_i b_i$. By linearity, and because $b_1, \ldots, b_k \in ker(f)$:

     $$y = f\left( \sum_{i=1}^{n}c_i b_i \right) = \sum_{i=1}^{k}c_i f(b_i) + \sum_{i=k+1}^n c_i f(b_i) = \sum_{i=k+1}^n c_i f(b_i)$$

     Thus, the set $S$ spans $im(f)$.

  4. Proof of Linear Independence

     Assume $\sum_{i=k+1}^n c_i f(b_i) = 0$. By linearity, $f(\sum_{i=k+1}^n c_i b_i) = 0$, meaning the vector $v = \sum_{i=k+1}^n c_i b_i$ is in $ker(f)$. Hence,

     Because $v \in ker(f)$, it can be expressed using the kernel's basis: $v = \sum_{i=1}^k c_i b_i$. Hence,

     $$\sum_{i=1}^k c_i b_i - \sum_{i=k+1}^{n} c_i b_i = 0$$

     Because $\left\{ b_1, \ldots, b_n \right\}$ is a basis of $V$, all coefficients must be strictly zero. Therefore, $c_{k+1} = \ldots = c_n = 0$, proving $S$ is linearly independent.

  5. Conclusion

     Therefore, as $S$ spans $im(f)$ and is linearly independent, it is a basis of $im(f)$. The size of this basis is $n-k$.

     Therefore, $dim(V) = dim(ker(f)) + dim(im(f))$.
