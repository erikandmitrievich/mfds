* Problem 1 :implicit_function_theorem:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Let $U \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ be open and define

$$f(x, y) = x^3 + x - 2y$$

(a) Use the Implicit function Theorem to argue that near every point $(a, b) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ with $f(a, b) = 0$, the equation

$$f(x, y) = 0$$

defines $y$ as a continuously differentiable function $y = g(x)$.

(b) Next, determine $g$.
#+end_quote

** Solution
*** (a)

1. $f(x,y) = x^3 + x - 2y$ is a polynomial, hence $\mathcal{C}^{\infty}$, hence $\mathcal{C}^{1}$.

2. Here $n = m = 1$. $\frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = -2$ ($1 \times 1$ matrix)

3. $-2 \neq 0$, so $\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(a,b)$ is invertible at every point.

4. Therefore [[file:../../01_material/ch_07_analysis_in_multivariable_coordinates.org::#thm:implicit-function][Theorem 7.5.1]] applies at each point $(a,b)$ with $f(a,b) = 0$. Locally $y = g(x)$ exists and is $\mathcal{C}^1$.

*** (b)

$$x^3 + x - 2y = 0 \implies g(x) = \frac{x^3 + x}{2}$$

defined and $\mathcal{C}^1$ on all of $\mathbb{R}$ — global, exceeding the IFT's local guarantee.

* Problem 2 :inverse_function_theorem:jacobian:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Let
$$f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2, \quad f(x_1, x_2) = \begin{pmatrix} e^{x_1} \cos x_2 \\ e^{x_1} \sin x_2 \end{pmatrix}$$

(a) Compute $f'(x)$.

(b) Show that $f'(x)$ is invertible for every $x \in \mathbb{R}^2$.

(c) Use the inverse function theorem to conclude that $f$ is locally invertible near every point $x \in \mathbb{R}^2$.

(d) Is $f$ globally injective on $\mathbb{R}^2$?
#+end_quote

** Solution
*** (a)

\begin{align*}
f(x_1, x_2) &= \begin{pmatrix} e^{x_1} \cos x_2 \\ e^{x_1} \sin x_2 \end{pmatrix} \\
f'(x_1, x_2) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{x_1} \cos x_2 & - e^{x_1} \sin x_2 \\
e^{x_1} \sin x_2 & e^{x_1} \cos x_2
\end{pmatrix} \\
\end{align*}

*** (b)

\begin{align*}
\mathrm{det}(f'(x)) &= (e^{x_1} \cos x_2)(e^{x_1} \cos x_2) - (-e^{x_1} \sin x_2)(e^{x_1} \sin x_2) \\
&= e^{2x_1} (\cos^2(x_2) + \sin^2(x_2)) \\
&= e^{2x_1} \\
\end{align*}

Since $e^{2x_1} > 0 \, \forall x_1 \in \mathbb{R}$, the determinant is strictly positive, so $f'(x)$ is invertible $\forall x \in \mathbb{R}^2$.

*** (c)

The [[file:../../01_material/ch_07_analysis_in_multivariable_coordinates.org::#thm:implicit-function][Theorem 7.5.1]] hypotheses hold at every $x \in \mathbb{R}^2$:

- domain $\mathbb{R}^2$ is open,

- $f$ is continuously differentiable ($\mathcal{C}^{\infty}$),

- $f'(x)$ is invertible (by (b), $\det f'(x) = e^{2x_1} > 0$).

By the [[file:../../01_material/ch_07_analysis_in_multivariable_coordinates.org::#thm:implicit-function][Theorem 7.5.1]], $f$ restricts to a bijection on some neighborhood of every $x$ — i.e. $f$ is locally invertible near every point. The inverse is continuously differentiable with

$$(f^{-1})'(f(x)) = (f'(x))^{-1}.$$

*** (d)

No, $f$ is not globally injective. $f$ is $2\pi$ -periodic in $x_2$:

$$f(x_1, x_2 + 2\pi) = \begin{pmatrix} e^{x_1} \cos(x_2 + 2\pi) \\ e^{x_1} \sin(x_2 + 2\pi) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} e^{x_1} \cos x_2 \\ e^{x_1} \sin x_2 \end{pmatrix} = f(x_1, x_2).$$

* Problem 3 :submanifold:tangent_space:normal_space:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Let

$$M_n = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : \|x\|_2 = 1\}$$

(a) Show that $M_n$ is a $n-1$ -dimensional submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^n$.

(b) Determine the tangent space $T_x(M_n)$ and the normal space $N_x(M_n)$ for all $x \in M_n$.

(c) Visualize $M_2$, $T_x(M_2)$, and $N_x(M_2)$ in Julia for the point $x = (1, 0)^\top$.
#+end_quote

** Solution
*** (a)

Let $g = f : \mathbb{R}^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$, $g(x) = \|x\|_2^2 - 1$, $g \in \mathcal{C}^{1}$. Here $k = n - 1$, so $r = n - k = 1$.

Take $U = \mathbb{R}^n$. Then $M_n \cap U = \{ x : g(x) = 0 \} = M_n$.

Gradient: $g'(x) = 2x^{\top}$. For $x \in M_n$, $\|x\| = 1$ so $x \neq 0$, hence $g'(x) \neq 0$ and $\operatorname{rank} g'(x) = 1 = r$.

By [[file:../../01_material/ch_05_analysis_in_coordinates.org::#def:submanifold][Definition 5.4.1]], $M_n$ is an $n - 1$ dimensional submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^n$.

*** (b)

Tangent space = kernel of the derivative:

$$T_x(M_n) = \ker f'(x) = \{ v \in \mathbb{R}^n : \langle 2x, v \rangle = 0 \} = \{ v \in \mathbb{R}^n : \langle x, v \rangle = 0 \} = x^{\perp}.$$

Hyperplane through the origin orthogonal to $x$. $\dim = n - 1$.

Normal space = orthogonal complement:

$$N_x(M_n) = (T_x M_n)^{\perp} = \operatorname{span}\{x\} = \{ \lambda x : \lambda \in \mathbb{R} \}.$$

Spanned by the gradient direction. $\dim = 1$.

Both are linear subspaces (through $0$). Drawn geometrically they attach at $x$ as affine lines.

*** (c)

At $x = (1, 0)^{\top}$: $T_x M_2 = \{ (0, t) \}$ (vertical), $N_x M_2 = \{ (s, 0) \}$ (the $x_1$ axis). Attached at $x$: tangent is the vertical line $x_1 = 1$, normal is the $x_1$ axis.

#+begin_src julia :results value :exports both :tangle julia/p_3c.jl

# M_2: unit circle

x = [1.0, 0.0]                     # point on M_2
tang_dir = [0.0, 1.0]              # spans T_x(M_2):  <x, v> = 0
norm_dir = [1.0, 0.0]              # spans N_x(M_2):  span{x}

     label = "M₂", aspect_ratio = :equal, lw = 2, legend = :bottomleft)

      label = "Tₓ(M₂)", lw = 2, ls = :dash)

      label = "Nₓ(M₂)", lw = 2, ls = :dot)
scatter!([x[1]], [x[2]]; label = "x = (1,0)", ms = 6)
xlabel!("x₁"); ylabel!("x₂")

* Problem 4 :submanifold:jacobian:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Let

$$M = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^3 : g_1(x) = 0, g_2(x) = 0\}, \quad g_1(x) = x_1^2 + x_2^2 - 1, \quad g_2(x) = x_3 - x_1$$

(a) Compute the Jacobian $g'(x)$ for $g = (g_1, g_2)^\top$.

(b) Show that $M$ is a 1-dimensional submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^3$.
#+end_quote

** Solution
*** (a)

\begin{align*}
g(x) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
x_1^2 + x_2^2 - 1 \\
x_3 - x_1
\end{pmatrix} \\
J_g(x) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
2x_1 & 2x_2 & 0 \\
-1 & 0 & 1
\end{pmatrix} \\
\end{align*}

*** (b)

$k = 1$, $n = 3 \implies r = n - k = 2$. Take $U = \mathbb{R}^3$; then $M \cap U = \{x : g(x) = 0\} = M$, and $g \in \mathcal{C}^{1}$.

$$g'(x) = \begin{pmatrix} 2x_1 & 2x_2 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

For a two row matrix, $\operatorname{rank} = 2$ iff the rows are linearly independent. Row $2 = (-1, 0, 1) \neq 0$. If $\text{row}_1 = \lambda \cdot \text{row}_2$, column $3$ gives $0 = \lambda$, forcing $\text{row}_1 = 0$, i.e. $x_1 = x_2 = 0$. But on $M$, $g_1 = 0$ gives $x_1^2 + x_2^2 = 1$, so $x_1, x_2$ are never both zero.

Hence the rows are independent and $\operatorname{rank} g'(x) = 2 = r$ for all $x \in M$. By [[file:../../01_material/ch_07_analysis_in_multivariable_coordinates.org::#def:submanifold][Definition 7.5.3]], $M$ is a $1$ dimensional submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^3$.
