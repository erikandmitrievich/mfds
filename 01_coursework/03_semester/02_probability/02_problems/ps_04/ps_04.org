* Problem 1 :uniform_law:transformation:density:
** Task
#+begin_quote
*Conceptual: an exact value and a rounded display.* Let $X$ be an idealized measurement uniformly distributed on $[0, 1]$. We choose the density $f_X(x) \coloneqq 1$ for every $x \in (0, 1)$ and $f_X(x) \coloneqq 0$ otherwise. Let $Y$ be the value obtained by rounding $X$ to the nearest hundredth; if $X$ is exactly halfway between two such values, round upward. For example, the unrounded value $0.3718$ produces the displayed value $0.37$. Thus $X$ is the unrounded measurement, whereas $Y$ is the number on the display.

(a) Suppose the display reads $0.37$. Determine the interval of possible values of $X$, including which endpoints belong to the interval. Express the event $\{Y = 0.37\}$ in terms of $X$, and sketch the interval on a number line. Use the sketch to explain whether the display determines $X$ exactly.

(b) Now consider a new measurement, before observing its display. Under the stated uniform model, calculate

\[ F_X(0.37) = P(X \leq 0.37), \quad P(X = 0.37), \quad P(Y = 0.37), \quad f_X(0.37) \]

These are unconditional quantities: do not condition on the display reading $0.37$. Show each calculation and explain what the quantity describes.

(c) Use the interval from part (a) and the calculations from part (b) to explain why $P(Y = 0.37) > 0$ and $P(X = 0.37) = 0$ do not contradict each other. Distinguish the density value $f_X(0.37)$ from the probability obtained by integrating $f_X$ over that interval.
#+end_quote

** Solution

*** (a)

Ties round up, so $0.365 \mapsto 0.37$ but $0.375 \mapsto 0.38$. The possible values of $X$ form the interval $[0.365, 0.375)$, and

\[ \{Y = 0.37\} = \{0.365 \leq X < 0.375\} \]

#+begin_example
        0.365       0.37        0.375
  --------[===========+===========)--------> x
#+end_example

Every value in the segment is displayed as $0.37$, so the display does not determine $X$: it only tells us that $X$ lies in this interval of length $0.01$.

*** (b)

\begin{gather*}
F_X(0.37) = \int_0^{0.37} 1 \, dx = 0.37 \\
P(X = 0.37) = \int_{0.37}^{0.37} 1 \, dx = 0 \\
P(Y = 0.37) = P(0.365 \leq X < 0.375) = \int_{0.365}^{0.375} 1 \, dx = 0.01 \\
f_X(0.37) = 1
\end{gather*}

- $F_X(0.37)$: probability that the measurement is at most $0.37$.
- $P(X = 0.37)$: probability that the measurement is exactly $0.37$.
- $P(Y = 0.37)$: probability that the display reads $0.37$.
- $f_X(0.37)$: probability per unit length near $0.37$.

*** (c)

No contradiction: $\{X = 0.37\}$ fixes $X$ at a single point, while $\{Y = 0.37\} = \{0.365 \leq X < 0.375\}$ only confines it to an interval of length $0.01$ containing that point. The display reading $0.37$ does not mean $X = 0.37$.

The density value $f_X(0.37) = 1$ is not a probability; probabilities come from integrating $f_X$ over a set. Over the point $\{0.37\}$ (length $0$) the integral is $0$; over $[0.365, 0.375)$ (length $0.01$) it is $1 \cdot 0.01 = 0.01$.
