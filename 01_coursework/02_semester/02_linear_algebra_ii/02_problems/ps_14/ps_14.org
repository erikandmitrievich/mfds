* Problem 5.22 :diagonalization:linear_map:polynomial:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Let $\mathbb{E} = \mathbb{R}_4[X]$, and let $L : \mathbb{E} \to \mathbb{E}$ be defined as $L(P) := P' + P''$. Determine whether $L$ is diagonalizable.
#+end_quote

** Solution

$\mathbb{E} = \mathbb{R}_4[X]$ is the space of polynomials of degree $\le 4$, so $\dim(\mathbb{E}) = 5$ with standard basis $B = (1, X, X^2, X^3, X^4)$.

Applying $L(P) = P' + P''$ to each basis vector,

\begin{align*}
L(1) &= 0 + 0 \\
L(X) &= 1 + 0 = 1 \\
L(X^2) &= 2X + 2 = 2 + 2X \\
L(X^3) &= 3X^2 + 6X = 6X + 3X^2 \\
L(X^4) &= 4X^3 + 12X^2 = 12X^2 + 4X^3
\end{align*}

gives the matrix

\begin{align*}
A = [L]_B^B = \begin{pmatrix}
0 & 1 & 2 & 0 & 0  \\
0 & 0 & 2 & 6 & 0  \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 3 & 12 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 4  \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align*}

$A$ is strictly upper triangular, so $A - t\,\mathrm{Id}$ is upper triangular with diagonal $-t$. By [[file:../../01_material/ch_04_determinants.org::#prop:triangular-matrix-determinant][Proposition 4.4]],

#+begin_quote
$$\det(A) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} a_{i,i}.$$
#+end_quote

$$p_L(t) = \det(A - t\,\mathrm{Id}) = (-t)^5 = -t^5$$

so by [[file:../../01_material/ch_05_eigenvalues.org::#lem:characteristic-polynomial][Lemma 5.9]] (3) the only eigenvalue is $\lambda = 0$.

#+begin_quote
$$p_f(\lambda) = 0 \iff \lambda \text{ is an eigenvalue of } f.$$
#+end_quote

Suppose $L$ is diagonalizable. By [[file:../../01_material/ch_05_eigenvalues.org::#lem:diagonalizable-similar-diagonal][Lemma 5.13]], $A = PDP^{-1}$ for some diagonal $D$.

#+begin_quote
2. $A$ is diagonalizable if and only if it is similar to a diagonal matrix, namely, there exists an invertible matrix $P$ and a diagonal matrix $D \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$ such that

   $$A = PDP^{-1}$$
#+end_quote

The diagonal entries of $D$ are its eigenvalues, which by [[file:../../01_material/ch_05_eigenvalues.org::#cor:similar-matrices-eigenpairs][Corollary 5.4]] are also eigenvalues of $A$, hence all $0$. So $D = 0$, giving $A = P\,0\,P^{-1} = 0$ and thus $L = 0$ — contradicting $L(X) = 1 \neq 0$.

#+begin_quote
Let $A$ and $B \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$ be two similar matrices such that $B = PAP^{-1}$ for an invertible matrix $P \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$. Then, if $(\lambda, x) \in \mathbb{K} \times (\mathbb{K}^n \setminus \{0\})$ is an eigenpair of $A$, then $(\lambda, Px)$ is an eigenpair of $B$.
#+end_quote

Therefore $L$ is not diagonalizable.
