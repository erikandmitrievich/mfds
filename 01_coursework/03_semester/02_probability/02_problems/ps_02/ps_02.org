* Problem 1 :counting:uniform_law:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Conceptual. A program claims to choose a subset of $\{1, \dots, 10\}$ uniformly. It first chooses its size uniformly from $\{0, 1, \dots, 10\}$, then chooses uniformly among the subsets of that size. For a fixed subset $S$ with $|S| = k$, derive $\mathbb{P}(S \text{ is selected})$. Compare this probability for $k = 0, 1, 5$ with the value $2^{-10}$ required by a uniform choice among all subsets. Compare the induced distribution of subset size under the two procedures, and propose a correct algorithm for choosing uniformly among all $2^{10}$ subsets.
#+end_quote

** Solution

The program picks the size $K$ uniformly, so $\mathbb P(K=k)=\frac1{11}$. Given $K=k$, each of the $\binom{10}{k}$ subsets of size $k$ is equally likely. For $|S|=k$,

\[ \mathbb P(S \text{ selected}) = \mathbb P(K=k)\,\mathbb P(S \mid K=k) = \frac{1}{11\binom{10}{k}}. \]

Compare with $2^{-10} = 1/1024 \approx 0.00098$:

| k | C(10,k) | P(S)             | ratio to 2⁻¹⁰ |
|---+---------+------------------+---------------|
| 0 |       1 | 1/11 ≈ 0.0909    | ≈ 93          |
| 1 |      10 | 1/110 ≈ 0.0091   | ≈ 9.3         |
| 5 |     252 | 1/2772 ≈ 0.00036 | ≈ 0.37        |

Small subsets are heavily over-selected and mid-sized ones under-selected, so the program is not uniform.

*Size distribution.* Under the program, $\mathbb P(K=k)=\frac1{11}$ for every $k$. Under a uniform choice of subset, $\mathbb P(K=k)=\binom{10}{k}2^{-10}$, which is $\mathrm{Bin}(10,\tfrac12)$: sizes near $5$ dominate ($\mathbb P(K=5)\approx 0.246$) and $\mathbb P(K=0)=2^{-10}$.

*Correct algorithm.* For each $i\in\{1,\dots,10\}$, independently flip a fair coin and include $i$ on heads. A fixed $S$ requires a specific outcome on each of the 10 flips, so $\mathbb P(S \text{ selected}) = 2^{-10}$ for every $S$ .

* Problem 2 :counting:inclusion_exclusion:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Mathematical.

(a) An eight-character identifier is generated uniformly from 26 uppercase letters, 26 lowercase letters, and 10 digits, with replacement. How many identifiers contain at least one character from each of the three categories? State the corresponding probability and justify the inclusion-exclusion terms.

(b) Let $A_1, \dots, A_M \subseteq \Omega$. Prove the pointwise identity

\[ \mathbf{1}_{\bigcup_{s=1}^{M} A_s}(\omega) = \sum_{\emptyset \neq J \subseteq \{1, \dots, M\}} (-1)^{|J|+1} \mathbf{1}_{\bigcap_{s \in J} A_s}(\omega) \]

Hint. If $\omega$ belongs to exactly $h$ of the events, show that the right-hand side equals

\[ \sum_{k=1}^{h} (-1)^{k+1} \binom{h}{k} = 1 \]

Deduce the general inclusion-exclusion formulas for cardinalities and probabilities.
#+end_quote

** Solution
*** (a)

The alphabet has $62$ characters, so there are $62^8$ identifiers in total. Let $U$, $L$, $D$ be the sets of identifiers containing *no* uppercase letter, *no* lowercase letter and *no* digit, respectively. We want $62^8 - |U \cup L \cup D|$.

Each term counts strings drawn from a restricted alphabet, with every position chosen independently:

- $|U| = |L| = 36^8$ (only $26+10$ characters allowed), $|D| = 52^8$;

- $|U\cap L| = 10^8$ (digits only), $|U \cap D| = |L \cap D| = 26^8$ (one letter case only);

- $|U\cap L\cap D| = 0^8 = 0$ (no characters allowed).

By inclusion-exclusion,

\begin{align*}
N &= 62^8 - (2\cdot 36^8 + 52^8) + (10^8 + 2\cdot 26^8) - 0 \\
  &= 159\,655\,911\,367\,680,
\end{align*}

\[ \mathbb P(\text{all three categories}) = \frac{N}{62^8} \approx 0.731. \]

*** (b)

Fix $\omega$ and let $H = \{s : \omega \in A_s\}$, $h = |H|$. Then $\mathbf 1_{\bigcap_{s\in J}A_s}(\omega) = 1$ iff $J \subseteq H$.

If $h = 0$, both sides are $0$. If $h \geq 1$, only the nonempty $J\subseteq H$ contribute, and there are $\binom hk$ of them with $|J| = k$, so

\[ \text{RHS} = \sum_{k=1}^{h} (-1)^{k+1}\binom hk = 1 - \sum_{k=0}^{h}(-1)^k\binom hk = 1 - (1-1)^h = 1 = \text{LHS}. \]

*Cardinalities.* For finite $A_s$, sum the identity over $\omega \in \bigcup_s A_s$, using $\sum_\omega \mathbf 1_B(\omega) = |B|$:

\[ \Big|\bigcup_{s=1}^M A_s\Big| = \sum_{\emptyset\neq J} (-1)^{|J|+1}\Big|\bigcap_{s\in J}A_s\Big|. \]

*Probabilities.* For $A_s \in \mathcal F$, take expectations of both sides, using linearity and $\mathbb E[\mathbf 1_B] = \mathbb P(B)$:

\[ \mathbb P\Big(\bigcup_{s=1}^M A_s\Big) = \sum_{\emptyset\neq J} (-1)^{|J|+1}\,\mathbb P\Big(\bigcap_{s\in J}A_s\Big). \]

* Problem 3 :counting:inclusion_exclusion:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Python/data. A data set has 15 labeled rows, four of which are flagged. A five-row test set is selected uniformly without replacement. Use ~itertools.combinations~ to enumerate all test sets and compute the exact probability that at least two flagged rows are selected. Verify the result with a combinatorial formula and with Monte Carlo simulation.
#+end_quote

** Solution

$X$ = number of flagged rows in the test set. Choosing $k$ of the 4 flagged and $5-k$ of the 11 unflagged rows,

\[ \mathbb P(X \ge 2) = \sum_{k=2}^{4} \frac{\binom4k\binom{11}{5-k}}{\binom{15}5} = \frac{990+220+11}{3003} = \frac{37}{91} \approx 0.4066. \]

By symmetry the choice of flagged rows is irrelevant; take =A=--=D=.

#+begin_src python :results output
import itertools
import random
from fractions import Fraction

rows = "ABCDEFGHIJKLMNO"
flagged = set("ABCD")

sets = list(itertools.combinations(rows, 5))
print(Fraction(sum(len(flagged & set(s)) >= 2 for s in sets), len(sets)))

random.seed(0)
n = 100_000
p = sum(len(flagged & set(random.sample(rows, 5))) >= 2 for _ in range(n)) / n
print(f"{p:.4f} +/- {1.96 * (p * (1 - p) / n) ** 0.5:.4f}")
#+end_src

Enumeration matches the formula; $37/91$ lies in the 95% Monte Carlo interval.
