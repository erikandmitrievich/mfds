* Problem 1 :subspace:
** Task
#+begin_quote
*Conceptual.* Take $\Omega = \mathbb{R}$, where an outcome $\omega$ is a temperature measured in degrees Celsius. A sensor answers only the two questions

\[ A = \{ \omega \in \Omega \mid \omega < 18 \}, \quad B = \{ \omega \in \Omega \mid \omega < 22 \} \]

Find a sigma-algebra containing $A$ and $B$ with as few sets as possible, and justify why no smaller sigma-algebra can work. Is $\{20\}$ an event in this sigma-algebra?
#+end_quote

** Solution

Atoms (disjoint, cover $\mathbb{R}$):

\[ A = (-\infty,18), \quad B\setminus A = [18,22), \quad B^c = [22,\infty) \]

\[ \sigma(A,B) = \{\text{unions of atoms}\} = \{\emptyset,\ A,\ B\setminus A,\ B^c,\ B,\ A\cup B^c,\ A^c,\ \mathbb{R}\}, \quad 2^3 = 8 \text{ sets} \]

Minimal: any \(\sigma\)-algebra $\ni A, B$ contains $B^c$ and $B \cap A^c = B\setminus A$, hence all unions of atoms $\Rightarrow$ $\geq 8$ sets. The family above is closed under unions and complements (complement = union of the remaining atoms) $\Rightarrow$ it is a \(\sigma\)-algebra.

$\{20\}$: no. $20 \in [18,22)$, and every set in $\sigma(A,B)$ contains either all of $[18,22)$ or none of it .

* Problem 2 :projection:
** Task
#+begin_quote
*Mathematical.* Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ be a probability space and let $A, B \in \mathcal{F}$. Starting from the definition of a probability space, prove

\[ \mathbb{P}(A \setminus B) = \mathbb{P}(A) - \mathbb{P}(A \cap B) \]

and use it to derive the two-event union formula

\[ \mathbb{P}(A \cup B) = \mathbb{P}(A) + \mathbb{P}(B) - \mathbb{P}(A \cap B) \]
#+end_quote

** Solution

$A\setminus B = A \cap B^c \in \mathcal{F}$.

Finite additivity: \(\sigma\)-additivity with $A_n = \emptyset$ for $n \geq 3$, using $\mathbb{P}(\emptyset) = 0$.

\[ A = (A\setminus B) \sqcup (A\cap B) \]

\begin{align*}
\implies \mathbb{P}(A) &= \mathbb{P}(A\setminus B) + \mathbb{P}(A\cap B) \\
\implies \mathbb{P}(A\setminus B) &= \mathbb{P}(A) - \mathbb{P}(A\cap B)
\end{align*}

\[ A\cup B = (A\setminus B) \sqcup B \]

\begin{align*}
\implies \mathbb{P}(A\cup B) &= \mathbb{P}(A\setminus B) + \mathbb{P}(B) \\
&= \mathbb{P}(A) - \mathbb{P}(A\cap B) + \mathbb{P}(B) \\
&= \mathbb{P}(A) + \mathbb{P}(B) - \mathbb{P}(A\cap B)
\end{align*}

* Problem 3 :subspace:
** Task
#+begin_quote
*Python/data.* Use ~lecture_01_examples.ipynb~ as a starting point. Let $\Omega = \{1, \dots, 6\}$ and $\mathcal{F} = 2^\Omega$. Choose nonnegative probabilities $p_1, \dots, p_6$, not all equal, that sum to one and define $\mathbb{P}(\{k\}) = p_k$ for $k = 1, \dots, 6$. Compute the model value of $\mathbb{P}(A)$ for $A = \{2, 4, 6\}$. For two different random seeds, simulate $n = 5000$ rolls and plot the relative frequency $\widehat{p}_m(A)$ based on the first $m$ rolls, for $m = 1, \dots, n$, together with $\mathbb{P}(A)$. Compare the deviations at $m = 50, 500, 5000$, explain the early and late fluctuations, and explain why the experiment illustrates but does not prove convergence.
#+end_quote

** Solution

Take $p = (0.08, 0.10, 0.12, 0.15, 0.20, 0.35)$. Since $A = \{2\}\cup\{4\}\cup\{6\}$ is a disjoint union,

$$\mathbb P(A) = p_2 + p_4 + p_6 = 0.60.$$

#+begin_src python :results output raw
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

faces = np.arange(1, 7)
probs = np.array([0.08, 0.10, 0.12, 0.15, 0.20, 0.35])
A, P_A, n = [2, 4, 6], 0.6, 5000
m = np.arange(1, n + 1)
checkpoints = (50, 500, 5000)
devs = {}

for seed in [1, 2]:
    rolls = np.random.default_rng(seed).choice(faces, size=n, p=probs)
    p_hat = np.cumsum(np.isin(rolls, A)) / m
    plt.plot(m, p_hat, lw=1, label=f"seed {seed}")
    devs[seed] = [abs(p_hat[k - 1] - P_A) for k in checkpoints]

plt.axhline(P_A, color="black", ls="--", label="P(A) = 0.6")
plt.xscale("log"); plt.xlabel("m"); plt.ylabel("relative frequency"); plt.legend()
plt.show()

print("| $m$ | seed 1 abs. dev | seed 2 abs. dev | $\\sqrt{0.24/m}$ |")
print("|-")
for i, k in enumerate(checkpoints):
    print(f"| {k} | {devs[1][i]:.3f} | {devs[2][i]:.3f} | {np.sqrt(0.24 / k):.3f} |")
#+end_src

#+RESULTS:
|  $m$ | seed 1 abs. dev | seed 2 abs. dev | $\sqrt{0.24/m}$ |
|------+-----------------+-----------------+-----------------|
|   50 |           0.060 |           0.000 |           0.069 |
|  500 |           0.012 |           0.016 |           0.022 |
| 5000 |           0.002 |           0.011 |           0.007 |

*Fluctuations.* One roll changes $\widehat p_m$ by at most $1/m$. For small $m$ the jumps are large and the two seeds differ visibly. For large $m$ the steps are tiny and the deviation shrinks roughly like $1/\sqrt m$, but it does not reach zero. Seed 2 has zero deviation at $m=50$ but $0.011$ at $m=5000$: along one path the deviation need not shrink monotonically; only its typical size does.

*Not a proof.* Convergence is a statement about the limit $m\to\infty$ over (almost) all sample paths. We see only finitely many rolls on two paths, and the simulation already assumes the model it is supposed to confirm .

* Problem 4 :subspace:
** Task
#+begin_quote
*Additional mathematical exercise: De Morgan's law.* Let $\Omega$ be a sample space and let $A_k \subseteq \Omega$ for every $k \in \mathbb{N}$. With complements taken relative to $\Omega$, prove

\[ \bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty} A_k = \left( \bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty} A_k^c \right)^c \]

Start with an arbitrary $\omega \in \Omega$ and express membership on each side using "for every" and "there exists." Then let $\mathcal{F}$ be a sigma-algebra on $\Omega$ with $A_k \in \mathcal{F}$ for every $k$. Use the identity and the definition of a sigma-algebra to show that $\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty} A_k \in \mathcal{F}$.
#+end_quote

** Solution

Let $\omega \in \Omega$. By definition,

\begin{align*}
\omega \in \bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty} A_k &\iff \text{for every } k,\ \omega \in A_k, \\
\omega \in \bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty} A_k^c &\iff \text{there exists } k \text{ with } \omega \notin A_k.
\end{align*}

The second statement is the negation of the first, so

\[ \omega \in \bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty} A_k \iff \omega \notin \bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty} A_k^c \iff \omega \in \left( \bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty} A_k^c \right)^c. \]

Since $\omega$ was arbitrary, the identity holds.

Now let $\mathcal{F}$ be a \(\sigma\)-algebra with $A_k \in \mathcal{F}$ for all $k$. Then

\begin{align*}
A_k^c &\in \mathcal{F} \text{ for all } k && \text{(closed under complements)} \\
\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty} A_k^c &\in \mathcal{F} && \text{(closed under countable unions)} \\
\left( \bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty} A_k^c \right)^c &\in \mathcal{F} && \text{(closed under complements)}
\end{align*}

By the identity, the last set equals $\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty} A_k$, so $\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty} A_k \in \mathcal{F}$. $\blacksquare$
