* Problem 1 :optimization:lagrange_multipliers:hessian:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Consider $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ given by

$$f(x, y) = (x + 1)^2 + 3y^2$$

(a) Determine all local extrema of $f$ and check by the Hessian if it is a local minimum or local maximum.

(b) Consider the constraint problem $g(x) = 0$, for which $g : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ given by

$$g(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 1$$

Compute all local constraint extrema.

(c) Check by the Hessian of the Lagrangian if each local constraint extremum is a local constraint maximum or minimum.

(d) Visualize $f$ and $g$ is Julia, so that you "see" the local (constraint) extrema.

(e) Parameterize the circle by $\gamma : [0, 1] \to \mathbb{R}^2$,

$$\gamma(t) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos(2\pi t) \\ \sin(2\pi t) \end{pmatrix}$$

and plot the function $t \mapsto f(\gamma(t))$. Does this plot confirm the local constraint extrema?
#+end_quote

** Solution
*** (a)

Critical points:
\begin{align*}
\nabla f(x, y)
&= \begin{pmatrix} f_x \\ f_y \end{pmatrix}
 = \begin{pmatrix} 2x + 2 \\ 6y \end{pmatrix}
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
\nabla f = 0
\iff
\begin{cases}
2x + 2 = 0 \\
6y = 0
\end{cases}
\iff
(x, y) = (-1, 0)
\end{align*}

Hessian:
\begin{align*}
H_f(x, y)
&= \begin{pmatrix} f_{xx} & f_{xy} \\ f_{yx} & f_{yy} \end{pmatrix} \\
&= \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 6 \end{pmatrix}
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
\begin{pmatrix} x_1 & x_2 \end{pmatrix}
H_f
\begin{pmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \end{pmatrix}
&=
\begin{pmatrix} 2x_1 & 6x_2 \end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \end{pmatrix} \\
&= 2x_1^2 + 6x_2^2 > 0
\quad \forall (x_1, x_2) \neq (0, 0)
\end{align*}

Hence $H_f$ is positive definite, so $(-1, 0)$ is a local minimum.

*** (b)

Constraint $g(x,y) = x^2 + y^2 - 1$. Lagrange condition ([[file:../../01_material/ch_05_analysis_in_coordinates.org::#cor:lagrange-multipliers][Corollary 5.4.7]]): $\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g$.

\begin{align*}
\nabla f(x,y) = \begin{pmatrix} 2x + 2 \\ 6y \end{pmatrix} \quad
\nabla g(x,y) = \begin{pmatrix} 2x \\ 2y \end{pmatrix}
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
\begin{pmatrix} 2x + 2 \\ 6y \end{pmatrix} &= \lambda \begin{pmatrix} 2x \\ 2y \end{pmatrix} \\
&\implies \begin{cases}
2x + 2 = 2 \lambda x \\
6y = 2 \lambda y &\implies (\lambda = 3 \text{ or } y = 0) \\
x^2 + y^2 - 1 = 0 \\
\end{cases} \\
\end{align*}

- *Case 1: $y \neq 0$*

  \begin{align*}
  &\begin{cases}
  2x + 2 = 6x &\implies (x = \frac{1}{2})\\
  x^2 + y^2 - 1 = 0 \\
  \end{cases} \\
  &\implies (\frac{1}{2})^2 + y^2 - 1 = 0 \iff y = \pm \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\
  \end{align*}

  Hence, local extremas are: $\Big(\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\Big), \Big(\dfrac{1}{2}, -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\Big)$

- *Case 2: $y = 0$*

  \begin{aligned}
  \begin{cases}
  2x + 2 = 2 \lambda x & \implies
      \begin{cases}
      x = 1 &\implies \lambda = 2 \\
      x = -1 &\implies \lambda = 0
      \end{cases} \\
  x^2 = 1 & \implies x = \pm 1
  \end{cases}
  \end{aligned}

  Hence, local extremas are: $(1, 0), (-1, 0)$

The complete list of local constraint extrema is:

$$\Big(\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\Big), \Big(\dfrac{1}{2}, -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\Big), (1, 0), (-1, 0)$$

*** (c)

Recall $\lambda$:
\begin{align*}
\Big(\frac{1}{2}, \pm\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\Big): \quad \lambda = 3 \\
(1, 0): \quad \lambda = 2 \\
(-1, 0): \quad \lambda = 0 \\
\end{align*}

$$H_g = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$$

Hessian of the Lagrangian in $x$ (with $L = f - \lambda g$):

\begin{align*}
H_x L(x, y, \lambda) &= H_f - \lambda H_g \\
&= \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 6 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 2\lambda & 0 \\ 0 & 2\lambda \end{pmatrix} \\
&= \begin{pmatrix} 2 - 2\lambda & 0 \\ 0 & 6 - 2\lambda \end{pmatrix} \\
\end{align*}

*Point $(-1, 0)$ with $\lambda = 0$:*

\begin{align*}
H_xL(-1, 0, 0) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 0 \\
0 & 6
\end{pmatrix}
\end{align*}

By (a), it is classified as local minimum.

*Point $(1, 0)$ with $\lambda = 2$:*

\begin{align*}
H_x L(1, 0, 2) &= \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \\
\nabla g(1, 0) &= (2, 0)^{\top} \\
T_p = \nabla g^{\perp} &= \mathrm{span}\{(0, 1)^{\top}\}
\end{align*}

For $v = (0, 1)^{\top}$:

$$v^{\top} (H_x L) v = 2 > 0$$

Positive definite on $T_p$ $\implies$ local constraint *minimum*, $f = 4$ (Second-order sufficient conditions under constraint).

*Points $\Big(\frac{1}{2}, \pm\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\Big)$ with $\lambda = 3$:*

\begin{align*}
H_x L\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}, \pm\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, 3\Big) &= \begin{pmatrix} -4 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \\
\nabla g\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}, \pm\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\Big) &= (1, \pm\sqrt{3})^{\top} \\
T_p = \nabla g^{\perp} &= \mathrm{span}\{(\sqrt{3}, \mp 1)^{\top}\}
\end{align*}

For $v = (\sqrt{3}, \mp 1)^{\top}$:

$$v^{\top} (H_x L) v = -4(\sqrt{3})^2 + 0 = -12 < 0$$

Negative definite on $T_p$ $\implies$ local constraint *maximum*, $f = \frac{9}{2}$.

*Result.*

\begin{align*}
\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}, \pm\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\Big) &: \text{constraint maxima}, \quad f = \tfrac{9}{2} \\
(1, 0) &: \text{constraint minimum}, \quad f = 4 \\
(-1, 0) &: \text{constraint minimum}, \quad f = 0
\end{align*}

#+begin_quote
Let $(X, d_X)$ and $(Y, d_Y)$ be metric spaces and let $f : X \to Y$ be a function. Show that $f$ is continuous if and only if $f^{-1}(U)$ is open for all open sets $U \subseteq Y$.

(Hint: write the $\varepsilon\delta$ definition of continuity in terms of balls)
#+end_quote

- Setup

  $f$ is continuous at $x_0$ iff $\forall \varepsilon > 0 \; \exists \delta > 0 : f(B_\delta(x_0)) \subseteq B_\varepsilon(f(x_0))$, and continuous iff continuous at every $x \in X$.

  /Claim./ $f$ continuous $\iff f^{-1}(U)$ open in $X$ for every open $U \subseteq Y$.

- ($\Rightarrow$)

  Let $U \subseteq Y$ be open and $x_0 \in f^{-1}(U)$. Then $f(x_0) \in U$, so $\exists \varepsilon > 0$ with $B_\varepsilon(f(x_0)) \subseteq U$. By continuity at $x_0$, $\exists \delta > 0$ with $f(B_\delta(x_0)) \subseteq B_\varepsilon(f(x_0)) \subseteq U$, hence $B_\delta(x_0) \subseteq f^{-1}(U)$. So $f^{-1}(U)$ is open.

- ($\Leftarrow$)

  Let $x_0 \in X$ and $\varepsilon > 0$. The ball $B_\varepsilon(f(x_0))$ is open, so $f^{-1}(B_\varepsilon(f(x_0)))$ is open by hypothesis and contains $x_0$. Hence $\exists \delta > 0$ with $B_\delta(x_0) \subseteq f^{-1}(B_\varepsilon(f(x_0)))$, i.e. $f(B_\delta(x_0)) \subseteq B_\varepsilon(f(x_0))$. Thus $f$ is continuous at $x_0$; as $x_0$ was arbitrary, $f$ is continuous.

$C \mapsto Y \setminus C$ is a bijection between closed and open subsets of $Y$, and $f^{-1}(Y \setminus C) = X \setminus f^{-1}(C)$. So:

\begin{align*}
f \text{ continuous}
&\iff f^{-1}(U) \text{ open} \quad \forall\, U \text{ open} \\
&\iff f^{-1}(Y \setminus C) \text{ open} \quad \forall\, C \text{ closed} \\
&\iff X \setminus f^{-1}(C) \text{ open} \quad \forall\, C \text{ closed} \\
&\iff f^{-1}(C) \text{ closed} \quad \forall\, C \text{ closed}. \qquad \square
\end{align*}
