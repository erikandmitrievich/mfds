* Problem 5.3 :diagonalization:eigenvalue:spectral_shift:
** Task
#+begin_quote
(Shift of spectrum)

Let $A \in \mathbb{K}^{n\times n}$ and $\alpha \in \mathbb{K}$, show that $\alpha \cdot \mathrm{Id}_n + A$ is diagonalizable if and only if $A$ is diagonalizable. What are the eigenvalues and associated eigenspaces of $\alpha \cdot \mathrm{Id}_n + A$?
#+end_quote

** Solution

For $x \neq 0$, $(\alpha \cdot \mathrm{Id}_n + A)x = (\alpha + \lambda)x \iff Ax = \lambda x$. So the eigenvectors of the two matrices coincide, and the eigenvalues of $\alpha \cdot \mathrm{Id}_n + A$ are the shifts $\alpha + \lambda$ of those of $A$.

*Diagonalizability.* By [[file:../../01_material/ch_05_eigenvalues.org::#lem:diagonalizable-similar-diagonal][Lemma 5.13]], $A = PDP^{-1}$ with $D$ diagonal. Then

$$\alpha \cdot \mathrm{Id}_n + A = P(\alpha \cdot \mathrm{Id}_n + D)P^{-1},$$

and $\alpha \cdot \mathrm{Id}_n + D$ is diagonal, so $\alpha \cdot \mathrm{Id}_n + A$ is diagonalizable via the same $P$. Reversing: $\alpha \cdot \mathrm{Id}_n + A = PD'P^{-1}$ gives $A = P(D' - \alpha \cdot \mathrm{Id}_n)P^{-1}$. $\square$

*Eigenvalues and eigenspaces.* If $\sigma(A) = \{\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_k\}$, then $\sigma(\alpha \cdot \mathrm{Id}_n + A) = \{\alpha + \lambda_1, \ldots, \alpha + \lambda_k\}$, and

$$V_{\alpha+\lambda_i}^{\alpha \cdot \mathrm{Id}_n + A} = \ker\!\left((\alpha \cdot \mathrm{Id}_n + A) - (\alpha + \lambda_i)\,\mathrm{Id}_n\right) = \ker(A - \lambda_i \cdot \mathrm{Id}_n) = V_{\lambda_i}^{A}.$$

* Problem 5.5 :diagonalization:eigenvalue:matrix:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Let $A$ be the $n \times n$ matrix defined by

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} \alpha & 1 & 1 & 1 & \ldots & 1 \\ 1 & \alpha & 1 & 1 & \ldots & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & \alpha & 1 & \ldots & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 & \alpha & \ldots & 1 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & \ldots & \alpha \end{pmatrix}$$

Is $A$ diagonalizable over $\mathbb{R}$? If so, obtain the diagonalized form. (*Indication:* ex. 4.14 or ex. 5.3 may be of help.)
#+end_quote

** Solution

Write $A = (\alpha - 1)\,\mathrm{Id}_n + J$, where $J \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is the all-ones matrix ($J_{ij} = 1$).

By /Problem 5.3/, $A$ is diagonalizable iff $J$ is, and the eigenvalues of $A$ are those of $J$ shifted by $\alpha - 1$ (eigenspaces unchanged). It remains to diagonalize $J$.

*Spectrum of $J$.* Since $J = \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}$ with $\mathbf{1} = (1, \ldots, 1)^{\top}$, we have $\mathrm{rank}(J) = 1$.

- $\ker J = \{x : \sum_i x_i = 0\}$, of dimension $n - 1$: eigenvalue $0$, geometric multiplicity $n - 1$.

- $J\mathbf{1} = n\,\mathbf{1}$: eigenvalue $n$, eigenvector $\mathbf{1}$, geometric multiplicity $1$.

The geometric multiplicities sum to $(n-1) + 1 = n = \dim \mathbb{R}^n$, so by [[file:../../01_material/ch_05_eigenvalues.org::#thm:diagonalization-endomorphism][Theorem 5.20]] (point 3) $J$ is diagonalizable.

*Eigenstructure of $A$.* By /Problem 5.3/, $A$ is diagonalizable with eigenvalues shifted by $\alpha - 1$:

- $\alpha + n - 1$, multiplicity $1$, eigenspace $\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{1})$.

- $\alpha - 1$, multiplicity $n - 1$, eigenspace $\{x : \sum_i x_i = 0\}$.

*Diagonalized form.* $A$ is diagonalizable over $\mathbb{R}$ for every $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$, with

$$D = \mathrm{diag}(\alpha + n - 1, \underbrace{\alpha - 1, \ldots, \alpha - 1}_{n - 1}),$$

via $P = (\mathbf{1} \mid v_2 \mid \cdots \mid v_n)$, where $v_2, \ldots, v_n$ is any basis of the hyperplane $\sum_i x_i = 0$ (e.g. $e_1 - e_k$ for $k = 2, \ldots, n$). Then $A = PDP^{-1}$.
