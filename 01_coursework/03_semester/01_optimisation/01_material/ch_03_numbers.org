* Definition:  Idealized floating point numbers
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: def:idealized-fp
:LECTURE_REF: 3.3.1
:LLM: claude-opus-4-8
:END:

For a positive integer $m$, the idealized floating point numbers are

\[ \mathbb{R}^{[m]} \coloneqq \bigcup_{\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}} \{ \pm(1 + k2^{-m})2^\alpha \mid k = 0, 1, \dots, 2^m - 1 \} \cup \{0\} \]

The idealized machine precision is

\[ \operatorname{eps}^{[m]} \coloneqq 2^{-m} \]

* Lemma:       Idealized rounding error bound
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: lem:idealized-rounding-error-bound
:LECTURE_REF: 3.3.2
:LLM: claude-opus-4-8
:END:

For every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, the mapping $\operatorname{fl}^{[m]}\colon \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^{[m]}$ satisfies

\[ \lvert \operatorname{fl}^{[m]}(x) - x \rvert \leq \operatorname{eps}^{[m]} \lvert x \rvert \tag{3.3} \]

#+begin_proof
Consider $x > 0$. There is $\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that $x \in [2^\alpha, 2^{\alpha+1})$. The definition of $\operatorname{fl}^{[m]}$ yields

\[ \lvert \operatorname{fl}^{[m]}(x) - x \rvert \leq \lvert 2^{\alpha - m} \rvert = 2^{-m} 2^\alpha \leq \operatorname{eps}^{[m]} \lvert x \rvert \]

The inequality also holds for $x = 0$ and for $x < 0$ we derive

\[ \lvert \operatorname{fl}^{[m]}(x) - x \rvert = \lvert -\operatorname{fl}^{[m]}(x) + x \rvert = \lvert \operatorname{fl}^{[m]}(-x) - (-x) \rvert \]

so that the estimate for $-x > 0$ applies. $\blacksquare$
#+end_proof

* Theorem:     Fundamental Theorem of Computational Precision
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: thm:fundamental-computational-precision-eps
:LECTURE_REF: 3.3.3
:LLM: claude-opus-4-8
:END:

For $x \in \mathbb{R}$, there is $\lvert \varepsilon \rvert \leq \operatorname{eps}$ such that

\[ \operatorname{fl}^{(\operatorname{eps})}(x) = x(1 + \varepsilon) \tag{3.10} \]

For $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^{(\operatorname{eps})}$, there is $\lvert \varepsilon \rvert \leq \operatorname{eps}$ such that

\[ x * y = (x \star y)(1 + \varepsilon) \tag{3.11} \]

#+begin_proof
We may assume $x \neq 0$ and define $\varepsilon = \frac{\operatorname{fl}^{(\operatorname{eps})}(x) - x}{x}$. Then (3.10) holds and according to the axiom of computation we have

\[ \lvert \varepsilon \rvert = \left\lvert \frac{\operatorname{fl}^{(\operatorname{eps})}(x) - x}{x} \right\rvert \leq \operatorname{eps} \]

The claim (3.11) follows analogously. $\blacksquare$
#+end_proof

* Definition:  Floating point numbers
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: def:floating-point-numbers-64
:LECTURE_REF: 3.3.5
:LLM: claude-opus-4-8
:END:

The floating point numbers (for the 64-bit IEEE standard) are

\[ \mathbb{R}_{64} \coloneqq \bigcup_{\alpha=-1022}^{1023} \left\{ \pm\left(1, a_1 a_2 \dots a_{52}\right)_2 \cdot 2^\alpha \mid a_k \in \{0, 1\} \right\} \]

and we define

\[ R_{64} \coloneqq \max(\mathbb{R}_{64}), \qquad r_{64} \coloneqq \min(\{ x \in \mathbb{R}_{64} \mid x > 0 \}) \]

*** TODO Why from $\alpha = -1022$ to $1023$?
:PROPERTIES:
:LLM: claude-opus-5
:END:

Bit budget. A double has 64 bits: 1 sign, 11 exponent, 52 mantissa (the leading 1 is implicit, hence $(1, a_1 \dots a_{52})_2$).

The 11 exponent bits hold $e \in \{0, 1, \dots, 2047\}$, stored with bias $1023$, so $\alpha = e - 1023$ would range over $-1023, \dots, 1024$. The two extreme patterns are reserved:

- $e = 0$: zero and subnormal numbers (leading bit $0$ instead of $1$),
- $e = 2047$: $\pm\infty$ and NaN.

Left over: $e \in \{1, \dots, 2046\}$, i.e. $\alpha \in \{-1022, \dots, 1023\}$.

Consequence for the definition: since $e = 0$ is excluded, $\mathbb{R}_{64}$ as written contains neither $0$ nor the subnormals, so it is exactly the set of /normalized/ doubles. That makes $r_{64} = 2^{-1022}$ the smallest positive normalized number, and $R_{64} = (1, 1 \dots 1)_2 \cdot 2^{1023} = (2 - 2^{-52}) \cdot 2^{1023}$.

* Theorem:     Truncation of idealized floating point numbers
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: thm:truncation-idealized-fp-64
:LECTURE_REF: 3.3.6
:LLM: claude-opus-4-8
:END:

The set $\mathbb{R}_{64}$ is

\[ \mathbb{R}_{64} = \bigcup_{\alpha=-1022}^{1023} \{ \pm(1 + k2^{-52})2^\alpha \mid k = 0, 1, \dots, 2^{52} - 1 \} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{[52]} \tag{3.13} \]

so that it is a union of equally spaced numbers with gaps $2^{\alpha - 52}$.

#+begin_proof
The binary representation in (3.12) yields

\[ \left(1, a_1 a_2 \dots a_{52}\right)_2 = 1 + \sum_{k=1}^{52} a_k 2^{-k} \]

and /Corollary 2.2.20/ implies

\[ \{ 1 + k\, 2^{-52} \mid k = 0, 1, \dots, 2^{52} - 1 \} = \left\{ 1 + \sum_{j=1}^{52} a_j 2^{-j} \mid a_j \in \{0, 1\} \right\} \]

This concludes the proof. $\blacksquare$
#+end_proof

* Lemma:       Largest and smallest positive floating point numbers
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: lem:largest-smallest-positive-fp-64
:LECTURE_REF: 3.3.7
:LLM: claude-opus-4-8
:END:

The largest and smallest positive floating point numbers are

\[ R_{64} = 2^{1024}\left(1 - 2^{-53}\right) > 10^{308}, \qquad r_{64} = 2^{-1022} < 3 \cdot 10^{-308} \]

#+begin_proof
The claim $r_{64} = 2^{-1022}$ is obvious. For $R_{64}$, we observe

\begin{align*}
R_{64} &= (1 + (2^{52} - 1)2^{-52})2^{1023} \\
       &= (2 - 2^{-52})2^{1023} \\
       &= (1 - 2^{-53})2^{1024}
\end{align*}
#+end_proof

* Theorem:     Precision of computer arithmetics
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: thm:precision-computer-arithmetics-64-eps
:LECTURE_REF: 3.3.11
:LLM: claude-opus-4-8
:END:

For all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}_{64}$ satisfying $\lvert x \star y \rvert \in [r_{64}, R_{64}]$, we have

\[ \left\lvert \frac{x * y - (x \star y)}{x \star y} \right\rvert \leq \operatorname{eps}_{64} \tag{3.16} \]

#+begin_proof
The assertion follows directly from (3.15) and (3.14). $\blacksquare$
#+end_proof

* Definition:  Machine numbers
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: def:machine-numbers-float64
:LECTURE_REF: 3.3.14
:LLM: claude-opus-4-8
:END:

The set of /machine numbers/ is

\[ \operatorname{Float}64 = \mathbb{R}_{64} \cup \mathbb{R}_{64,\operatorname{sub}} \cup \{ \pm 0, \pm \operatorname{Inf}, \operatorname{NaN} \} \]

where $\pm \operatorname{Inf} = \pm\infty$ and $\operatorname{NaN}$ is Not a Number such as $0/0$.
