#+begin_quote
Let $f : [0, 1] \to \mathbb{R}$ be given by

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{4}(x^2 + 1)$$

(a) Show that $f$ maps $[0, 1]$ into itself and is a contraction on $[0, 1]$.

(b) Deduce that $f$ has a unique fixed point and implement the fixed point iteration in Julia.
#+end_quote

** Solution

*** (a)

\begin{align*}
x \in [0,1] &\implies x^2 \in [0,1] \\
            &\implies x^2 + 1 \in [1,2] \\
            &\implies \dfrac{1}{4}(x^2 + 1) \in \left[ \dfrac{1}{4}, \dfrac{1}{2} \right] \\
\end{align*}

As $\left[ \dfrac{1}{4}, \dfrac{1}{2} \right] \in [0,1]$, $f$ maps $[0,1]$ to itself.


\begin{align*}
|f(x) - f(y)| &= \dfrac{1}{4}|x^2 + 1 - y^2 - 1| \\
              &= \dfrac{1}{4}|x - y||x + y| \\
              &\le \dfrac{1}{4} \cdot 2|x - y| \\
              &= \dfrac{1}{2} |x - y| \\
\end{align*}

Thus, $f$ is a contraction on $[0,1]$ with $L = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

function fixed_point(f, x = rand(), tol=1e-10)
    x_prev = x
    x_curr = f(x)

    while (abs(x_curr - x_prev) > tol)
        x_prev = x_curr
        x_curr = f(x_prev)
    end
    return x_curr
end

Consider $f : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}$,

$$f(x, y, z) = e^x + y^2 + z$$

subject to the constraints

$$\begin{align*}
g_1(x, y, z) &= x + y + z - 1 = 0, \\
g_2(x, y, z) &= x^2 + y^2 - z = 0.
\end{align*}$$

(a) Show that the feasible set $M = \{(x, y, z)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^3 : g_1(x, y, z) = 0, g_2(x, y, z) = 0\}$ is a 1-dimensional submanifold.

(b) Derive the Lagrange multiplier system

$$\nabla f(x, y, z) = \lambda \nabla g_1(x, y, z) + \mu \nabla g_2(x, y, z), \quad g_1(x, y, z) = 0, \quad g_2(x, y, z) = 0$$.

Define $g(x,y,z) = \begin{pmatrix} g_1(x,y,z) \\ g_2(x,y,z) \end{pmatrix}$

Choose $g : \mathbb{R}^3 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^2,\ g(x,y,z) = \begin{pmatrix} g_1(x,y,z) \\ g_2(x,y,z) \end{pmatrix}$. $g \in \mathcal{C}^1$, as $g_1, g_2$ are polynomials. $k = 1,\ r = n - k = 2$.

Let $U = \mathbb{R}^3$. $M \cap U = \{ g(x,y,z) = 0 \} = M$.

$$J_g(x,y,z) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 2x & 2y & -1 \end{pmatrix} \neq 0\ \forall x, y \in \mathbb{R}$$.

Let $x,y = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, then $2(-1/2)^2 - z = 0 \Rightarrow z = 1/2$ ($g_2$), but $-1/2 - 1/2 + 1/2 - 1 = -3/2 \neq 0$ ($g_1$), hence the only case where $J_g$ rows are linearly dependent ($x,y=-1/2$) is not in $M$. Thus, $\mathrm{rank}(J_g(x,y,z)) = 2$.

function newton_method(f, df, x0, tol=1e-6, maxiter=50)

using Plots

function myNewton(f, df, x0, tol, maxiter)
    xs = [float(x0)]
    for k in 1:maxiter
        x = xs[end]
        x_new = x - f(x) / df(x)
        push!(xs, x_new)
        abs(x_new - x) < tol && return (x_new, k, xs)

end

#+begin_quote
Answer the following questions:

1. What does the Newton iterations do for the linear system of equations $Ax - b = 0$, where $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ and $b \in \mathbb{R}^n$?

2. Find a sequence $(x_k)_{k\in\mathbb{N}} \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ that converges linearly towards some $x^* \in \mathbb{R}^n$ but not quadratically.

3. Find a sequence $(x_k)_{k\in\mathbb{N}} \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ that converges quadratically towards some $x^* \in \mathbb{R}^n$.

4. Visualize the two aforementioned sequence in Julia by using suitable plots with logarithmic axis labels.

5. Compute the gradient of $f(x) = \|Ax - b\|^2$ for $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ and $b \in \mathbb{R}^n$. Apply the Newton method to the gradient of $f$. What do you observe?
#+end_quote

** Solution

*** (1)

For $f(x) = Ax - b = 0$ with $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ invertable, the Jacobian is constant, $J_f(x) = A$. The Newton iteration is:

\begin{align*}
x_{k+1} &= x_k - A^{-1}(Ax_k - b) \\
        &= x_k - (A^{-1}Ax_k - A^{-1}b) \\
        &= x_k - (x_k - A^{-1}b) \\
        &= A^{-1}b \\
\end{align*}

So from any $x_0$, already $x_1 = A^{-1}b$: Newton converges in one step.

*** (2)

A sequence converges linearly to $x^{* }$ if $\|x_{k+1} - x^{* }\| \le C\|x_k - x^{*}\|$ for some fixed $C \in (0,1)$. Take $x_k = c^k \rightarrow 0$ for any $c \in (0,1)$:

$$\|x_{k+1} - x^{* }\| = c^{k+1} = c \cdot c^k = C\|x_k - x^{*}\|$$

*** (3)

$$\|x_{k+1} - x^*\| = c^{2^{k+1}} = \left(c^{2^k}\right)^2 = \|x_k - x^*\|^2$$

\begin{align*}
f(x) &= \| Ax - b \|^2 \\

f(x) &= x^{\top}A^{\top}Ax - 2b^{\top}Ax + \|b\|^2 \\
\nabla f(x) &= 2A^{\top}Ax - 2A^{\top}b \\
            &= 2A^{\top}(Ax - b) \\

\begin{align*}
J_{\nabla f} &= 2A^{\top}A

x_{k+1} &= x_k - (\nabla f(x))^{-1}(f(x)) \\

        &= (A^{\top}A)^{-1}A^{\top}b \\
