** PS02 P1  Continuous image of $[0,1]$ is path-connected
:PROPERTIES:
:REL:  METHOD
:CONF: med
:END:

*** Statement
Let $X$ be a metric space and $f : [0, 1] \to X$ continuous. Show that $f([0, 1]) \subseteq X$ is path-connected.

*** Professor's signal
"Problem 1 in problem [sheet] two, that's classic. I wouldn't find it super likely, but I could mention it. Maybe." (rec_02_02). Nikita: "P1: maybe".

*** Notes
Path-connectedness via continuity is the classical technique: if $f : [0,1] \to X$ is continuous and $a, b \in f([0,1])$, then preimages $t_a, t_b \in [0,1]$ are joined by the straight segment, and $f$ restricted to that segment is a continuous path from $a$ to $b$. The professor flagged this as "classic" — the method to internalize — but rated the specific problem only "maybe" for the exam. Treat the result as a building block for path-connectedness arguments elsewhere (e.g. PS02 P3, which is downplayed but uses the same idea).

*** Professor's signal

** PS04 P5  Taylor of $g$ agrees with linear / Hessian approximation of $f(x,y) = g(x+y)$
:PROPERTIES:
:REL:  EXAM
:CONF: high
:END:

*** Statement
Let $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$, $g : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ both $C^2$, with $f(x,y) = g(x+y)$. (a) Show the order-1 Taylor polynomial of $g$ at $0$, evaluated at $t = x+y$, equals the affine linear approximation of $f$ at $(0,0)$. (b) For $g(t) = e^t$, show the order-2 Taylor polynomial of $g$ at $0$, evaluated at $t = x+y$, equals $f(0,0) + \nabla f(0,0)\cdot(x,y)^\top + \tfrac{1}{2}(x,y)\, H_f(0,0)(x,y)^\top$.

*** Professor's signal
"Everything except for 4 is certainly relevant for the exam." (rec_01_02). Nikita: "P5: will be."

*** Notes
This is the conceptual bridge between 1D Taylor and the multivariate quadratic Taylor formula — exactly the perspective the professor returns to in PS06 P2 ("Taylor approximation of order 2, significance of the Hessian"). Mechanically: $\partial_x f = \partial_y f = g'(x+y)$, $\partial_x^2 f = \partial_y^2 f = \partial_x \partial_y f = g''(x+y)$, so $H_f = g''(x+y)\,\begin{pmatrix}1 & 1\\1 & 1\end{pmatrix}$. Then $(x,y) H_f(0,0) (x,y)^\top = g''(0)(x+y)^2$, which is exactly what falls out of the 1D Taylor of $g$ when one writes $t^2 = (x+y)^2$. Be prepared to do this kind of derivation cleanly in both directions.

** PS05 P2  Fréchet derivative of $L(x) = \|Ax - b\|^2$; normal equations
:PROPERTIES:
:REL:  EXAM
:CONF: high
:END:

*** Statement
$L : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$, $L(x) = \|Ax - b\|^2$. (a) Compute the Fréchet derivative. (b) Show $x$ is a global minimum iff $A^\ast A x = A^\ast b$. (c) Implement the affine local approximation in Julia.

*** Professor's signal
"Classic to give some function of the Fréchet derivative — that's very likely in one way or the other, maybe not exactly for this function." (rec_01_02). Nikita: "P2: very likely."

*** Notes
The professor flagged the kind, not necessarily this specific instance — expect a quadratic-loss / normal-equations item on the exam, possibly with a different matrix. Mechanically: $L(x+h) - L(x) = 2\langle Ax-b, Ah\rangle + \|Ah\|^2$, so $L'(x)h = 2\langle A^\ast(Ax-b), h\rangle$, i.e., $\nabla L(x) = 2 A^\ast(Ax - b)$. Global minimality follows because $L$ is convex (quadratic with $A^\ast A \succeq 0$), so $\nabla L(x) = 0 \Leftrightarrow A^\ast A x = A^\ast b$ characterizes global minima — the normal equations. Be ready to (i) derive the Fréchet derivative from first principles (do not skip the $o(\|h\|)$ bookkeeping), and (ii) explain the iff in (b) explicitly via convexity.

** PS05 P3  Chain rule $(g \circ f)'(x,y)$
:PROPERTIES:
:REL:  METHOD
:CONF: high
:END:

*** Statement
$f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$, $f(x,y) = (x^2, y^2)^\top$; $g : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$, $g(u,v) = u - v$. Compute $(g \circ f)'(x,y)$.

*** Professor's signal
"Chain rule for the Fréchet derivative — sure this can happen probably." (rec_01_02). Nikita: "P3: chain rule for Fréchet der."

*** Notes
The signal is on the technique. The exam-form delivery is most likely a different pair $(f, g)$, but the recipe is fixed: $(g \circ f)'(x) = g'(f(x))\, f'(x)$ as a product of Jacobians. The exam-shape risk: getting the multiplication order or shape wrong (Jacobian of outer evaluated at $f(x)$, applied to Jacobian of inner at $x$).

** PS05 P4  Jacobi matrix and linear approximation; estimate $f(1.01, 0.98)$
:PROPERTIES:
:REL:  METHOD
:CONF: high
:END:

*** Statement

The exam play: present (c) either via the multiplier-determined unique critical point + a boundary argument, or by direct AM-GM citing the textbook inequality.

** PS14 P3  Banach fixed point in $C([0,1])$: $\Phi(f)(t) = t + f(t)^2/4$
:PROPERTIES:
:REL:  EXAM
:CONF: high
:END:

*** Statement
$C([0,1])$ with $\|f\|_\infty$. $\Phi : C([0,1]) \to C([0,1])$, $\Phi(f)(t) = t + f(t)^2/4$. (a) $C([0,1])$ is a Banach space. (b) $\Phi'(f)$. (c) $\|\Phi'(f)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le 1/2$ for $\|f\|_\infty \le 1$. (d) $\Phi$ has a fixed point; determine it.

*** Professor's signal
"You should know that this space is a Banach space and that you can apply Banach's fixed point [theorem] in this space. For sure there will be something with Banach['s fixed-point theorem] on the exam." (rec_01_02 + rec_02_02). Nikita: "P3: should know Banach space / apply Banach fixed point thm" + "For sure something with Banach fixed point thm on the exam."

*** Notes
The single strongest exam guarantee on the entire problem set — the professor said "for sure" about the topic and named this exact problem as the rehearsal. The pieces fit together as the function-space promotion of the 1D contraction in PS02 P4: (a) standard — $C([0,1])$ with sup-norm is complete because uniform-Cauchy continuous functions converge uniformly to a continuous limit. (b) Fréchet derivative: $\Phi(f + h)(t) - \Phi(f)(t) = (f(t) + h(t))^2/4 - f(t)^2/4 = f(t)h(t)/2 + h(t)^2/4$. The linear part $h \mapsto f h / 2$ is the Fréchet derivative; the $h^2/4$ remainder is $o(\|h\|_\infty)$. So $\Phi'(f) : C([0,1]) \to C([0,1])$, $\Phi'(f)\cdot h = (fh)/2$. (c) $\|\Phi'(f) h\|_\infty = \sup_t |f(t) h(t)|/2 \le \|f\|_\infty \|h\|_\infty / 2 \le \|h\|_\infty/2$ when $\|f\|_\infty \le 1$, so $\|\Phi'(f)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le 1/2$. (d) On the closed ball $\{f : \|f\|_\infty \le 1\}$, $\Phi$ maps into itself ($|\Phi(f)(t)| \le 1 + 1/4 < 2$ — needs sharper check; in fact $\Phi(f)(t) \in [t, t + 1/4] \subset [0, 5/4]$ — strictly the invariance needs the ball $\{\|f\|_\infty \le 1\}$ to be checked, and the contraction estimate from (c) via the mean-value theorem gives $\|\Phi(f) - \Phi(g)\|_\infty \le \tfrac{1}{2}\|f - g\|_\infty$ — contraction). Banach gives a unique fixed point $f^\ast$ satisfying $f^\ast(t) = t + f^\ast(t)^2/4$, i.e. $f^\ast(t)^2 - 4 f^\ast(t) + 4t = 0$, so $f^\ast(t) = 2 - 2\sqrt{1 - t}$ (selecting the root that stays in $[0,1]$ at $t = 0$). Exam version: be ready to (i) state Banach's fixed-point theorem cleanly, (ii) verify invariance and contraction on the closed ball, (iii) extract the fixed point as the limit of the iteration, (iv) solve the algebraic equation for $f^\ast(t)$.
