* Problem 1 :lagrange:constrained_optimization:submanifold:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Let $f : (0,\infty)^n \to \mathbb{R}$, given by

$$f(x) = x_1 x_2 \cdots x_n,$$

and consider the constraint manifold

$$M = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : x_1 + \cdots + x_n = n\}$$.

(a) Show that $M$ is an $(n-1)$ -dimensional submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^n$.

(b) Show that every constrained extremum $x \in (0,\infty)^n$ satisfies

$$\nabla f(x) = \lambda(1, \dots, 1)$$.

(c) Prove that $f(x) \le 1$, $\forall x \in M \cap (0,\infty)^n$, and determine when equality occurs.
#+end_quote

** Solution
*** (a)

$M$ is an $(n-1)$ dimensional submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^n$ iff $\forall x_0 \in M$

- $\exists$ an open $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ with $x_0 \in U$

- $g \in \mathcal{C}^1(U, \mathbb{R}^r)$, $r = n-(n-1) = 1$, such that

  $$M \cap U = \{x \in U : g(x) = 0\}, \qquad \mathrm{rank}\, g'(x) = r = 1 \quad \forall x \in M \cap U.$$

*Proof.*

Define $g \colon \mathbb{R}^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$, $g(x) = x_1 + \cdots + x_n - n$.

$g$ is defined on all of $\mathbb{R}^n$, so $U = \mathbb{R}^n$ serves as one global neighborhood: open, containing every $x_0$, discharging the $\forall x_0$ at once.

Hence,

1. $g$ is polynomial, hence $g \in \mathcal{C}^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R})$.

2. $M \cap U = M = g^{-1}(0)$ by definition of $M$.

3. $g'(x) = (1, \ldots, 1)$, so $\mathrm{rank}\, g'(x) = 1 = r$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$, in particular on $M$.

All conditions hold. Hence $M$ is an $(n-1)$ dimensional submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^n$.

*** (b)

*Proof.*

Apply [[file:../../01_material/ch_05_analysis_in_coordinates.org::#cor:lagrange-multipliers][Corollary 5.4.7]] with:

- $U = (0,\infty)^n$, open in $\mathbb{R}^n$. (not $\mathbb{R}^n$, the extremum is constrained to $(0,\infty)^n$)

- $r = 1$, $g_1 = g$, $g(x) = x_1 + \cdots + x_n - n$.

- $M \cap U = \{x \in U : g(x) = 0\}$, an $(n-1)$ dimensional submanifold: the (a) argument restricts to $U$ unchanged, since $\mathrm{rank}\, g'(x) = 1$ for all $x \in U$.

- $f \in \mathcal{C}^1(U)$: $f$ is polynomial.

Given $f\rvert_M$ has a local extremum at $x_0 \in M \cap U$, the corollary yields $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$ with

$$\nabla f(x_0) = \lambda \nabla g(x_0).$$

Since $\nabla g(x_0) = (1, \ldots, 1)$,

$$\nabla f(x_0) = \lambda(1, \ldots, 1)$$

*** (c)

*Proof.*

Let $x^\ast = (1, \ldots, 1)$, $f(x^\ast) = 1$.

By (b), $\partial_i f(x) = f(x)/x_i = \lambda \;\forall i \Rightarrow$ all $x_i$ equal $\Rightarrow x = x^\ast$ (using $\sum x_i = n$). Unique critical point.

$\Delta = \{x_i \ge 0,\ \sum x_i = n\}$ is compact, $f$ continuous, so $f$ attains a max on $\Delta$. On $\partial\Delta$: some $x_i = 0$, so $f = 0$; interior: $f > 0$. Max lies in the interior $\Rightarrow$ critical point $\Rightarrow x^\ast$. Hence $\max_\Delta f = 1$.

Therefore $f(x) \le 1$ on $M \cap (0,\infty)^n$, equality iff $x = (1, \ldots, 1)$.

* Problem 3 :lagrange:hessian:constrained_optimization:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Consider $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ given by

$$f(x) = x_1^2 + 2x_2^2$$

subject to the constraint

$$g(x) = x_1^2 + x_2^2 - 1 = 0$$.

Use the Lagrangian and its Hessian to identify the local minimum of the restriction of $f$. Visualize your result in Julia.
#+end_quote

** Solution

*Lagrangian.* $L(x, \lambda) = f(x) - \lambda g(x) = x_1^2 + 2x_2^2 - \lambda(x_1^2 + x_2^2 - 1)$.

*Stationarity.* $\nabla_x L = 0$ and $g = 0$:

\begin{align*}
\partial_1 L &= 2(1 - \lambda) x_1 = 0 \\
\partial_2 L &= 2(2 - \lambda) x_2 = 0 \\
g &= x_1^2 + x_2^2 - 1 = 0
\end{align*}

$x_1, x_2$ cannot both vanish (violates $g = 0$), and $\lambda$ cannot be both $1$ and $2$. Two cases:

- $\lambda = 1 \implies x_2 = 0 \implies x_1 = \pm 1$: points $(\pm 1, 0)$, $f = 1$.

- $\lambda = 2 \implies x_1 = 0 \implies x_2 = \pm 1$: points $(0, \pm 1)$, $f = 2$.

*Hessian of $L$ in $x$.*

\begin{align*}
H_x L =
\begin{pmatrix} \partial_{11} L & \partial_{12} L \\ \partial_{21} L & \partial_{22} L \end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix} 2 - 2\lambda & 0 \\ 0 & 4 - 2\lambda \end{pmatrix}
\end{align*}

*Classification on the tangent space.* The constraint is the unit circle, so $T_x = \ker g'(x) = x^{\perp}$ (/Definition 7.5.3/). Restrict $H_x L$ to $T_x$:

- $(\pm 1, 0)$, $\lambda = 1$: $T_x = \mathrm{span}\{(0,1)\}$, $H_x L = \mathrm{diag}(0, 2)$. For $v = (0, t)$, $v^{\top} H_x L\, v = 2t^2 > 0$ — positive definite on $T_x$ $\implies$ local *minimum*.

- $(0, \pm 1)$, $\lambda = 2$: $T_x = \mathrm{span}\{(1,0)\}$, $H_x L = \mathrm{diag}(-2, 0)$. For $v = (t, 0)$, $v^{\top} H_x L\, v = -2t^2 < 0$ — negative definite on $T_x$ $\implies$ local *maximum*.

*Result.* The restriction of $f$ to $g = 0$ has local minima at $(\pm 1, 0)$ with $f = 1$ (and maxima at $(0, \pm 1)$ with $f = 2$).

*Check.* On $g = 0$: $f = x_1^2 + 2x_2^2 = (x_1^2 + x_2^2) + x_2^2 = 1 + x_2^2$, minimal at $x_2 = 0$, maximal at $x_2^2 = 1$. $\square$

* Problem 4 :gradient_descent:quadratic_form:convergence:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Consider $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ given by

$$f(x, y) = x^2 + 2xy + 5y^2$$.

(a) Show that $f$ has a unique global minimizer.

(b) Write the gradient descent iteration

$$u_{k+1} = u_k - \tau \nabla f(u_k), \quad u_k = (x_k, y_k)^\top, \quad \tau > 0,$$

in the form

$$u_{k+1} = A_\tau u_k,$$

where $A_\tau \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$.

(c) Determine all step sizes $\tau > 0$ for which $u_k \to 0$ for every initial vector $u_0 \in \mathbb{R}^2$.
#+end_quote

** Solution
*** (a)

\begin{align*}
\nabla f(x,y) &= \begin{pmatrix} 2x + 2y \\ 2x + 10y \end{pmatrix}
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
\nabla f(x,y) = 0 &\iff
\begin{cases} 2x + 2y = 0 \\ 2x + 10y = 0 \end{cases} \\
&\iff 8y = 0 \\
&\iff (x,y) = (0,0).
\end{align*}

Unique critical point $(0,0)$.

\begin{align*}
\partial_1 f    &= 2x + 2y  &  \partial_2 f    &= 2x + 10y \\
\partial_{11} f &= 2        &  \partial_{22} f &= 10 \\
\partial_{21} f &= 2        &  \partial_{12} f &= 2
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
H_f(x,y) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 2 \\
2 & 10
\end{pmatrix}
\quad \text{(constant)}
\end{align*}

$\det H_f = 16 > 0$ (definite) and $\partial_{11} f = 2 > 0$ (positive) $\Rightarrow$ $(0,0)$ local min.

$H_f$ constant and positive definite on $\mathbb{R}^2$ $\Rightarrow$ $f$ strictly convex $\Rightarrow$ $(0,0)$ unique global minimizer, $f(0,0) = 0$.

*** (b)

\begin{align*}
\nabla f(u) =
\begin{pmatrix} 2x + 2y \\ 2x + 10y \end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 10 \end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}
=: H_f\, u.
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}
u_{k+1} = u_k - \tau \nabla f(u_k) = u_k - \tau H_f\, u_k = (I - \tau H_f)\, u_k
\end{align*}

Hence $u_{k+1} = A_\tau u_k$ with

\begin{align*}
A_\tau = I - \tau H_f
&=
\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
- \tau
\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 10 \end{pmatrix} \\
&=
\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
-
\begin{pmatrix} 2\tau & 2\tau \\ 2\tau & 10\tau \end{pmatrix} \\
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
1 - 2\tau & -2\tau \\
-2\tau & 1 - 10\tau
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align*}

*** (c)
