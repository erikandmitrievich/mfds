* Problem 1 :constrained_optimization:lagrange_multipliers:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Find the extrema of $f : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}$,

$$f(x, y, z) = xyz$$

subject to the constraint

$$x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 12.$$
#+end_quote

** Solution

Constraint $g(x,y,z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 12$. Lagrange condition ([[file:../../01_material/ch_05_analysis_in_coordinates.org::#cor:lagrange-multipliers][Corollary 5.4.7]]): $\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g$.

$$\nabla f = (yz,\, xz,\, xy), \qquad \nabla g = (2x,\, 2y,\, 2z).$$

System:

$$yz = 2\lambda x, \quad xz = 2\lambda y, \quad xy = 2\lambda z, \quad x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 12.$$

Multiply the three stationarity equations by $x, y, z$ respectively:

$$xyz = 2\lambda x^2 = 2\lambda y^2 = 2\lambda z^2.$$

*Case $\lambda = 0$.* Then $yz = xz = xy = 0$, forcing at least two coordinates to vanish; $f = 0$. Not extremal.

*Case $\lambda \neq 0$.* Then $x^2 = y^2 = z^2$. Constraint gives $3x^2 = 12$, so $\lvert x \rvert = \lvert y \rvert = \lvert z \rvert = 2$. Hence $f = xyz = \pm 8$, the sign set by the parity of negative coordinates.

- $\max f = 8$ (even number of negatives): $(2,2,2),\ (2,-2,-2),\ (-2,2,-2),\ (-2,-2,2)$.

- $\min f = -8$ (odd number of negatives): $(-2,-2,-2),\ (-2,2,2),\ (2,-2,2),\ (2,2,-2)$.

* Problem 2 :constrained_optimization:lagrange_multipliers:ellipse:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Consider the ellipse

$$M = \{(x, y)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^2 : 4x^2 + y^2 = 16\}.$$

(a) Determine the points that are closest to and farthest from the origin.

(b) Visualize the problem in Julia.

(c) Determine the points on the ellipse that are closest to and farthest from $(3, 0)^\top$.
#+end_quote

** Solution
*** (a)

Extremize squared distance $d^2 = x^2 + y^2$ under $g = 4x^2 + y^2 - 16$ (the square root is monotone, so extrema coincide).

$$\nabla d^2 = (2x,\, 2y), \qquad \nabla g = (8x,\, 2y).$$

$$2x = 8\lambda x, \quad 2y = 2\lambda y \;\Longrightarrow\; x(1 - 4\lambda) = 0, \quad y(1 - \lambda) = 0.$$

Both coordinates cannot vanish (not on $M$).

- $x = 0 \Rightarrow y = \pm 4$, distance $4$.

- $y = 0 \Rightarrow x = \pm 2$, distance $2$.

Closest: $(\pm 2, 0)^\top$ at distance $2$ (ends of the minor axis). Farthest: $(0, \pm 4)^\top$ at distance $4$ (ends of the major axis).

*** (b)

scatter!(plt, [2, -2], [0, 0];   color=:green, ms=6, label="closest, d=2")

*** (c)

Extremize $(x - 3)^2 + y^2$ under the same $g$:

$$2(x - 3) = 8\lambda x, \quad 2y = 2\lambda y \;\Longrightarrow\; x(1 - 4\lambda) = 3, \quad y(1 - \lambda) = 0.$$

- $y = 0 \Rightarrow x = \pm 2$:  at $(2,0)^\top$, $d = 1$;  at $(-2,0)^\top$, $d = 5$.

- $\lambda = 1 \Rightarrow -3x = 3 \Rightarrow x = -1$, then $y^2 = 12$, so $(-1, \pm 2\sqrt{3})^\top$, $d^2 = 28$, $d = 2\sqrt{7}$.

Comparing $d^2 \in \{1,\ 25,\ 28\}$:

- Closest: $(2, 0)^\top$, $d = 1$.

- Farthest: $(-1, \pm 2\sqrt{3})^\top$, $d = 2\sqrt{7}$.

Along $\gamma(t) = (2\cos t,\, 4\sin t)$, $d^2(t) = -12\cos^2 t - 12\cos t + 25$ takes cyclic values $1,\ 28,\ 25,\ 28$, so $(-2,0)^\top$ is a *local minimum* ($d = 5$), not the farthest point.

* Problem 3 :constrained_optimization:lagrange_multipliers:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Find the extrema of $f : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}$,

$$f(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2$$

subject to the constraints

$$x + y + z = 6, \qquad x - y = 2.$$
#+end_quote

** Solution

Two constraints $g_1 = x + y + z - 6$, $g_2 = x - y - 2$. Lagrange with two multipliers: $\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g_1 + \mu \nabla g_2$, with $\nabla g_1 = (1,1,1)$, $\nabla g_2 = (1,-1,0)$.

$$2x = \lambda + \mu, \quad 2y = \lambda - \mu, \quad 2z = \lambda.$$

Sum of the first two: $x + y = \lambda$; with $2z = \lambda$ this gives $z = (x+y)/2$. Their difference: $x - y = \mu$, and the constraint $x - y = 2$ fixes $\mu = 2$.

Put $s = x + y$: then $z = s/2$ and $x + y + z = s + s/2 = 6 \Rightarrow s = 4$. So $x + y = 4$, $z = 2$; combined with $x - y = 2$, get $x = 3$, $y = 1$.

Point $(3, 1, 2)^\top$, $f = 9 + 1 + 4 = 14$. The constraint set is a line in $\mathbb{R}^3$, so $f$ (squared distance from the origin) is unbounded above and $14$ is the global *minimum*. No maximum.

* Problem 4 :constrained_optimization:lagrange_multipliers:parametrization:

*** 3.

From $F(t) = 4\cos 2t$: maximum $4$ at $t \in \{0, \pi\}$, i.e. $(\pm 2, 0)^\top$; minimum $-4$ at $t \in \{\pi/2,\, 3\pi/2\}$, i.e. $(0, \pm 2)^\top$.

Confirm via /Corollary 5.4.7/ with $g = x^2 + y^2 - 4$:

$$2x = 2\lambda x, \quad -2y = 2\lambda y \;\Longrightarrow\; x(1 - \lambda) = 0, \quad y(1 + \lambda) = 0.$$

- $x = 0 \Rightarrow y = \pm 2$, $f = -4$ (minimum).

- $\lambda = 1 \Rightarrow y = 0$, $x = \pm 2$, $f = 4$ (maximum).

$\max f = 4$ at $(\pm 2, 0)^\top$, $\min f = -4$ at $(0, \pm 2)^\top$ — matching the guess.
