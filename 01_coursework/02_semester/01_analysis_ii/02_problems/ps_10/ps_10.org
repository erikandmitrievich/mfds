* Problem 1 :newton_method:nonlinear_system:jacobian:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Given $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ by

$$f(x, y) = \begin{pmatrix} x^2 + y^2 - 1 \\ x - y \end{pmatrix}$$

(a) Determine all $(x, y)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^2$ such that $f(x, y) = 0$.

(b) By using the initial vector $X_0 = (1, \frac{1}{2})^\top \in \mathbb{R}^2$, apply the Newton iteration twice by hand to compute $X_2$. How big is the error?
#+end_quote

** Solution
*** (a)

Setting $f(x, y) = 0$ gives $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ and $x = y$. Substituting:

$$2x^2 = 1 \implies x = \pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$$

So the solutions are $\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^\top$ and $\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^\top$.

*** (b)

$$J_f(x, y) = \begin{pmatrix} 2x & 2y \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$$

**Iteration 1:**

\begin{align*}
X_0 &= \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1/2 \end{pmatrix} \\
f(X_0) &= \begin{pmatrix} 1/4 \\ 1/2 \end{pmatrix} \\
J_f(X_0) &= \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \\
[J_f(X_0)]^{-1} &= -\frac{1}{3} \begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \\
X_1 &= X_0 - [J_f(X_0)]^{-1} f(X_0) \\
&= \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1/2 \end{pmatrix} - \left[ -\frac{1}{3} \begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \right] \begin{pmatrix} 1/4 \\ 1/2 \end{pmatrix} \\
&= \begin{pmatrix} 3/4 \\ 3/4 \end{pmatrix}
\end{align*}

**Iteration 2:**

\begin{align*}
f(X_1) &= \begin{pmatrix} 1/8 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \\
J_f(X_1) &= \begin{pmatrix} 3/2 & 3/2 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \\
[J_f(X_1)]^{-1} &= -\frac{1}{3} \begin{pmatrix} -1 & -3/2 \\ -1 & 3/2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1/3 & 1/2 \\ 1/3 & -1/2 \end{pmatrix} \\
X_2 &= X_1 - [J_f(X_1)]^{-1} f(X_1) \\
&= \begin{pmatrix} 3/4 \\ 3/4 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 1/3 & 1/2 \\ 1/3 & -1/2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1/8 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \\
&= \begin{pmatrix} 17/24 \\ 17/24 \end{pmatrix}
\end{align*}

**Error:**

\begin{align*}
\|X_2 - X^*_1\|_\infty &= \left| \frac{17}{24} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \right| \approx 0.00122 \\
\|X_2 - X^*_1\|_2 &= \frac{17\sqrt{2}}{24} - 1 \approx 0.00173
\end{align*}

using LinearAlgebra, Test

function newton(f, df, x_0, tol=1e-6, maxiter=50)
    x_k = x_0
    x_k1 = x_0
    for i in 1:maxiter
        x_k1 = x_k - (df(x_k) \ f(x_k))
        if norm(x_k1 - x_k) < tol
            return x_k1
        end
        x_k = x_k1
    end
    return x_k1
end

* Problem 3 :newton_method:nonlinear_system:jacobian:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Consider $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ given by

$$f(x, y) = \begin{pmatrix} x^2 - y \\ y^2 - x \end{pmatrix}$$

(a) Find all solutions of $f(x, y) = 0$.

(b) Determine all points where the Jacobian of $f$ is not invertible and visualize those points in Julia.

(c) Investigate in Julia what happens when Newton's method is initialized near a point for which the Jacobian is not invertible.
#+end_quote

** Solution
*** (a)

Setting $f(x,y) = 0$ gives $x^2 = y$ and $y^2 = x$. Substituting $y = x^2$:

$$x^4 - x = 0 \implies x(x-1)(x^2+x+1) = 0$$

The only real roots are $x \in \{0, 1\}$. The corresponding $y = x^2$ yields the solutions:

$$f(x,y) = 0 \iff (x,y)^\top \in \left\{ (0,0)^\top,\ (1,1)^\top \right\}$$

*** (b)

The Jacobian is:

The Jacobian is

$$J_f(x,y) = \begin{pmatrix} 2x & -1 \\ -1 & 2y \end{pmatrix}$$

A $2 \times 2$ matrix is invertible iff its kernel is trivial. So $J_f(x,y)$ is not invertible iff there is $v = (a,b)^\top \neq 0$ with $J_f(x,y)\,v = 0$:

\begin{align*}
2x\,a - b &= 0 \\
-a + 2y\,b &= 0
\end{align*}

Row 1 gives $b = 2x\,a$. Substituting into row 2:

$$-a + 2y(2x\,a) = a(4xy - 1) = 0$$

If $a = 0$, then $b = 2x \cdot 0 = 0$, so $v = 0$. A nonzero kernel vector therefore exists iff $4xy - 1 = 0$.

$$J_f(x,y) \text{ not invertible} \iff 4xy = 1$$

$J_f$ is not invertible on the hyperbola $4xy = 1$.

*** (c)

On the diagonal $x = y$ Newton's iteration stays on the diagonal and reduces to scalar Newton for $g(x) = x^2 - x$. The singular point $x = 1/2$ is exactly the basin boundary:

$$x_0 < 1/2 \implies x_k \to 0, \qquad x_0 > 1/2 \implies x_k \to 1$$

A displacement across it flips the limit; started on the locus, the linear solve fails.

* Problem 4 :newton_method:convergence:gradient:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Answer the following questions:

1. What does the Newton iterations do for the linear system of equations $Ax - b = 0$, where $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ and $b \in \mathbb{R}^n$?

2. Find a sequence $(x_k)_{k\in\mathbb{N}} \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ that converges linearly towards some $x^* \in \mathbb{R}^n$ but not quadratically.

3. Find a sequence $(x_k)_{k\in\mathbb{N}} \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ that converges quadratically towards some $x^* \in \mathbb{R}^n$.

4. Visualize the two aforementioned sequence in Julia by using suitable plots with logarithmic axis labels.

5. Compute the gradient of $f(x) = \|Ax - b\|^2$ for $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ and $b \in \mathbb{R}^n$. Apply the Newton method to the gradient of $f$. What do you observe?
#+end_quote

** Solution
*** 1.

For $f(x) = Ax - b = 0$ with $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ invertible, the Jacobian is constant, $J_f(x) = A$. The Newton iteration is

\begin{align*}
x_{k+1} &= x_k - A^{-1}(Ax_k - b) \\
        &= x_k - A^{-1}Ax_k + A^{-1}b \\
        &= A^{-1}b
\end{align*}

So from any $x_0$, already $x_1 = A^{-1}b$: Newton converges in one step.

*** 2.

A sequence converges linearly to $x^*$ if $\|x_{k+1} - x^*\| \le C\|x_k - x^*\|$ for some fixed $C \in (0,1)$. Take $x_k = c^k \to 0$ for any $c \in (0,1)$:

$$\|x_{k+1} - x^*\| = c^{k+1} = c \cdot c^k = C\|x_k - x^*\|$$

*** 3.

A sequence converges quadratically to $x^*$ if $\|x_{k+1} - x^*\| \le C\|x_k - x^*\|^2$ for some fixed $C > 0$. Take $x_k = c^{2^k} \to 0$ for any $c \in (0,1)$:

$$\|x_{k+1} - x^*\| = c^{2^{k+1}} = \left(c^{2^k}\right)^2 = \|x_k - x^*\|^2$$

*** 5.

\begin{align*}
f(x) &= \|Ax - b\|^2 \\
&= x^{\top} A^{\top} A x - 2b^{\top} Ax + \|b\|^2 \\
\nabla f(x) &= 2A^{\top}Ax - 2A^{\top}b \\
&= 2A^{\top}(Ax - b)
\end{align*}

Applying Newton to $g(x) = \nabla f(x)$, whose Jacobian is $J_g = 2A^{\top}A$:

\begin{align*}
x_{k+1} &= x_k - (2A^{\top}A)^{-1} \cdot 2A^{\top}(Ax_k - b) \\
&= x_k - (A^{\top}A)^{-1}A^{\top}Ax_k + (A^{\top}A)^{-1}A^{\top}b \\
&= (A^{\top}A)^{-1}A^{\top}b
\end{align*}

Newton converges in one step to $(A^{\top}A)^{-1}A^{\top}b$. If $A$ is invertible, $(A^{\top}A)^{-1}A^{\top} = A^{-1}$ and this reduces to $A^{-1}b$.
