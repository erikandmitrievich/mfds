* Problem 5.6 :characteristic_polynomial:linear_map:isomorphism:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Let $\mathbb{E}$ be a vector space, and let $L, M \in \mathcal{L}(E)$ with $L$ being an isomorphism. Prove that $L \circ M$ and $M \circ L$ have the same characteristic polynomial.
#+end_quote

** Solution

Since $L$ is an isomorphism, $L^{-1} \circ L = \mathrm{Id}_E$, so

$$M \circ L = L^{-1} \circ (L \circ M) \circ L.$$

Subtract $t \mathrm{Id}_E = L^{-1} \circ (t \mathrm{Id}_E) \circ L$:

$$M \circ L - t \mathrm{Id}_E = L^{-1} \circ (L \circ M - t \mathrm{Id}_E) \circ L.$$

Apply $\det$ and use multiplicativity:

$$p_{M \circ L}(t) = \det(L^{-1}) \cdot \det(L \circ M - t \mathrm{Id}_E) \cdot \det(L) = p_{L \circ M}(t),$$

since $\det(L^{-1}) \cdot \det(L) = \det(L^{-1} \circ L) = 1$.

* Problem 5.8 :eigenvalue:eigenvector:diagonalization:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Let $p, q \in \mathbb{R}$ with $q \neq 0$. We consider the matrix

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} p & -q \\ q & p \end{pmatrix}$$.

Determine the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of $A$. Is $A$ diagonalizable over $\mathbb{R}$? and $\mathbb{C}$?
#+end_quote

** Solution

*Characteristic Polynomial.*

$$p_A(\lambda) = \mathrm{det}(A - \lambda I) = (p - \lambda)^2 + q^2$$

*Eigenvalues.*

Setting $p_A (\lambda) = 0$ ([[file:../../01_material/ch_05_eigenvalues.org::#lem:characteristic-polynomial][Lemma 5.9]]):

$$(p - \lambda)^2 = -q^2 \implies \lambda_{1,2} = p \pm iq$$

Since $q \neq 0$, both are non-real (complex conjugates).

*Eigenvectors.*

For $\lambda_1 = p + iq$:

$$A - \lambda_1 I = \begin{pmatrix} -iq & -q \\ q & -iq \end{pmatrix}$$

Row 1 gives $-iq \cdot x_1 - q \cdot x_2 = 0$, hence $x_2 = -i x_1$. Eigenvector $v_1 = (1, -i)^{\top}$.

For $\lambda_2 = p - iq$ (conjugate): $v_2 = (1, i)^{\top} = \overline{v_1}$.

*Diagonalizability.*

- Over $\mathbb{R}$: $p_A$ has no real roots (since $q \neq 0$), so $A$ has no real eigenvalues. *Not diagonalizable* over $\mathbb{R}$.

- Over $\mathbb{C}$: two distinct eigenvalues in a 2 dimensional space, hence two linearly independent eigenvectors. *Diagonalizable* over $\mathbb{C}$:

$$A = P D P^{-1}, \quad P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -i & i \end{pmatrix}, \quad D = \begin{pmatrix} p + iq & 0 \\ 0 & p - iq \end{pmatrix}.$$

*Geometric remark.*

$A$ acts on $\mathbb{R}^2$ as rotation by $\theta = \arg(p + iq)$ combined with scaling by $r = \sqrt{p^2 + q^2}$. A genuine rotation has no invariant real line (for $\theta \notin \{0, \pi\}$), which is exactly why no real eigenvector exists. Complex eigenvalues $p \pm iq$ are the polar form $re^{\pm i\theta}$ encoding the rotation-scaling.
